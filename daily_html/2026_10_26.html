PhysCreator: Creating Physically-Attributed 3D Assets at Will

Significant advancements in 3D asset creation have enabled high-fidelity 3D generation from images. However, most existing representations remain inherently static, lacking the ability to model physically plausible behaviors under dynamic interactions. We present \textbf{PhysCreator}, a unified framework that directly generates simulation-ready 3D representations with spatially varying physical properties from images. Unlike prior approaches that treat physical attributes as simple post-hoc predictions or rely on expensive per-instance optimization, we reformulate physical property estimation within a unified 3D representation learning framework. We introduce a voxel-aligned latent space by lifting image observations into a spatially structured 3D representation, in which geometry, appearance, and physical properties are jointly modeled and inferred in a feed-forward manner. To enable scalable learning without densely annotated physical ground truth, we leverage vision-language distillation to transfer material-aware semantic priors from powerful vision-language models into spatially grounded voxel-level supervision. Experiments demonstrate that our method achieves superior visual quality, physical plausibility, and generalization compared to prior approaches.

Task Relevance-Driven Prompt Expansion for Federated Continual Learning

Federated continual learning (FCL) has attracted considerable interest by enabling local clients to continually acquire knowledge from new tasks. However, most FCL methods assume that the global server has prior knowledge of the arrival times and number of categories for new tasks, which is unrealistic in the real world. Furthermore, they neglect the importance of positive inter-task semantic relationships when learning new tasks and are significantly affected by irrelevant relationships from old tasks, thereby exacerbating catastrophic forgetting. To resolve the above challenges, we develop a novel Task Relevance-driven Prompt Expansion (TRPE) model. Specifically, to eliminate the reliance on prior knowledge of new tasks, we propose an out-of-distribution detector to identify the arrival of new tasks within local clients, along with a radius-adaptive DBSCAN to infer the quantity of categories in new tasks on the proxy server. Moreover, to explore positive inter-task relationships, we calculate the affinity between the original gradient for learning new task and its gradient orthogonal to the feature space of old tasks to evaluate semantic relevance between old and new tasks from a global perspective. Then, we use this global task relevance to determine the key-prompt pair for new tasks and identify highly related old tasks. After concatenating their prompts for training, we can leverage such positive inter-task relations to improve the continual learning of newly arriving tasks. To balance the learning of new tasks and the forgetting of old ones, a soft orthogonality constraint is applied to the local key and prompt gradients. Experiments spanning diverse datasets and backbones confirm the efficacy of our TRPE model.

On Detection Reliability for Small Object Segmentation

This study investigates whether predicted bounding boxes are reliable for determining where small-object segmentation should be performed without risking false negatives outside those boxes. The investigation leverages adenovirus particles as small objects, YOLO26x for detection, YO-LO26x-Sem for segmentation, and Mask R-CNN for both detection and segmentation. Segmenta-tion-model predictions were matched to ground-truth adenovirus masks or reference objects using their centroids, with ground-truth mask centroids preferred over ground-truth bounding-box centroids as references for matching. Matched predicted-mask centroids were considered as inside at least one detector-predicted bounding box or outside all such boxes, while ground-truth masks without matched predictions were recorded as having no matched segmentation. For the YOLO26 models, 34 reference objects (47.89%) had matched segmentation centroids inside at least one pre-dicted box, 8 (11.27%) had centroids outside all predicted boxes, and 29 (40.85%) had no matched predicted segmentation. For the Mask R-CNN model, the corresponding counts were 69 (97.18%), 0 (0.00%), and 2 (2.82%). These preliminary experiments demonstrated that restricting segmentation to detector-predicted bounding boxes risks excluding matched segmentation predictions outside those boxes, potentially introducing false negatives.

No Cones, No Duals: A Fast and Scalable Solver for NRSfM

Non-rigid structure from motion (NRSfM) aims to recover camera motion and time-varying 3D shape from tracked 2D image measurements. Recent high-performing methods show that shape-space factorization remains highly effective for this task. These methods often use costly solvers for two key steps. First, a semidefinite programming (SDP) cone solver to perform Euclidean correction. Next, an alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) solver to recover the low-rank shape. These steps often scale poorly to long sequences and dense point tracks. We propose a fast, scalable NRSfM solver that avoids these costly steps. For rotation estimation, we introduce a differentiable Euclidean correction that directly optimizes small factors for each sequence. A row-polar projection then maps each estimated camera block to the closest row-orthonormal matrix. For shape estimation, we introduce an unrolled spectral-proximal solver. It combines reprojection-gradient steps with weighted singular-value shrinkage in the reshuffled shape domain. This solver further uses FISTA-style restart and continuation. The spectral shrinkage operator itself is classical, but we embed it in a fixed differentiable solver, while preserving NRSfM's geometric constraints. The proposed approach is category-agnostic, requires no external training data, and maps naturally to modern accelerator-friendly linear algebra primitives. Across sparse, dense, noisy, missing-data, and long-sequence benchmarks, our solver achieves substantial speedups with competitive reconstruction accuracy, including 7x-1000x runtime improvements on dense sequences with 28,887 tracked points.

AndroidFlux: Evaluating and Reward Modeling Failure Recovery Capabilities in Mobile Use Multimodal Agents

Mobile-use multimodal agents are typically evaluated by task success from a clean initial state, which does not isolate their ability to recover from intermediate errors. Because mobile environments change dynamically, a general mobile-use agent must complete tasks robustly even from unexpected states and in unseen environments. This requires knowing when and where an agent makes errors, and whether it can recover from errors and subsequent states. To evaluate this capability, we introduce AndroidFlux, a failure-recovery benchmark built on AndroidWorld. AndroidFlux restores recorded trajectories to controlled checkpoints before, at, and after the first recoverable error, so that different agents continue from the same device state and history. On these checkpoints, a complementary reward-modeling benchmark evaluates recovery-action preferences using downstream rollout outcomes. We further release an offline reinforcement learning (RL) dataset that improves both error recovery and overall task success. Our evaluations show that recovery performance only partially correlates with clean-start success, and that explicit reasoning helps only models trained with GUI-domain specific RL.

Spatially-Aware Self-Organizing Graph Autoencoders via Localized Hebbian Learning

Traditional autoencoders rely on static network architectures to learn latent representations. Recently we suggested a dynamic architecture inspired by Hebbian plasticity, enabling topological self-organization among convolutional feature maps (R. Koç et al., Proceedings of the SIU, 2026). Early implementations of this paradigm infer relationships among feature maps using global average pooling and model their interactions through scalar-valued connections. This approach falls short to fully exploit the underlying spatial information, embedded in input images. Furthermore, abrupt transitions between different loss functions and training dynamics can introduce representation shocks, leading to unstable feature learning. In this paper, we introduce a Spatially-Aware Self-Organizing Graph Autoencoder (S-LooM) to address these structural bottlenecks. By using spatial cross-correlation, and spatial kernels, S-LooM establishes dynamic connections among feature maps. We replace static scalar message passing of autoencoders with learnable spatial kernels and eliminate high-frequency deconvolutional artifacts observed in decoders using nearest-neighbor upsampling. Via a soft-blending training curriculum, the latent topology aligns smoothly during training. Our experiments show that S-LooM yields a more stable topology, compared to the state of the art dynamic architectures. Meaning, the graph structure that emerge has more resistance to feature entanglement and noise. It forms functional communities responsible for visual attributes, such as illumination, shape and texture even under high connection densities.

Authors: Robin Koç, Hazal Moğultay Özcan, Fatoş T. Yarman Vural

Birth-Death Structural Learning for 3D Gaussian Splatting

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has become a standard representation for real-time novel-view synthesis. Yet, its quality-efficiency trade-off still relies heavily on adaptive density control. Existing cloning, splitting, and pruning rules use proxy signals, such as position gradients, opacity, and image-space error. Although effective in practice, these signals do not directly answer the structural question behind density control: where should new Gaussians be allocated to reduce the multi-view reconstruction loss, and which existing primitives can be pruned with minimal penalty. To address this, we propose first-variation birth-death control for 3DGS, a principled approach that replaces heuristic structural decisions with a variational score derived from the reconstruction loss. At each structural update, we freeze the current Gaussian population, attach auxiliary mass coordinates to each primitive, and differentiate the multiview loss with respect to these coordinates. We demonstrate that this score is a coordinate of the first variation of the Splat Regression Model, giving a rigorous local descent surrogate for birth-death moves: high-score Gaussians are selected as birth sites, while low-score, low-opacity Gaussians are selected for removal. Across standard 3DGS benchmarks, integrating this score into 3DGS-MCMC yields state-of-the-art reconstruction quality and outperforms existing methods, delivering the most significant gains under tight Gaussian budgets. Ultimately, our work provides compelling evidence that gradient-based variational metrics offer a principled alternative to proxy heuristics in 3DGS.

CrossPair: Unlocking Generalist Visual Transformation Learning in Image Editors

Language models switch tasks from demonstrations, but vision still often relies on separate systems for restoration, editing, and stylization. A before--after example offers a shared instruction only if the transferable change can be separated from the scene that demonstrates it. We introduce CrossPair, which adapts a pretrained multimodal image editor to transfer diverse transformations from one exemplar pair. Exemplar-before, exemplar-after, and query images take fixed roles in its native VLM and VAE pathways: the VLM extracts operation semantics, while the VAE retains fine appearance and structure. A training-only, change-aware DINOv3 objective aligns feature-change directions across content-distinct scenes, guiding the output to follow the demonstrated relation without an inference-time encoder. We construct Transform-360K by grouping filtered pairs from conditional representations, restoration, editing, virtual try-on, and stylization under shared transformations. On Relation252K-derived seen and held-out transformations, \method improves target agreement and edit-direction similarity over released systems. Ablations show that the semantic and visual exemplar routes are complementary and that DINO relation alignment further strengthens transfer; Transform-360K training also improves aggregate held-out-operation scores.

TopX: Text-to-Motion for Unseen Skeletons from the Skeleton File Alone

Text-to-motion models generate for the skeletons they were trained on, while a new character arrives as a skeleton file and nothing else. We study text-to-motion for skeletons that no training clip has used, with no clips of the character, no template, no retargeting and no fine-tuning. We present TopX, a 73M-parameter flow-matching transformer that generates joint rotations and a root trajectory for any kinematic tree from its tree, rest pose and joint names. Motion is normalised by the rest pose and the size of the skeleton, and the rest pose enters as a world-frame descriptor that does not depend on how the rig's author oriented each joint, with rest conventions randomised in training. Attention is factorised over the joints and the frames of a motion and carries a spatial--temporal encoding designed for skeleton graphs of any topology: queries and keys are rotated across joints by a heat-kernel coordinate of the skeleton graph, which is defined for every tree and does not change with the order of its joints or the eigenbasis of its Laplacian, and across frames by a rotary encoding of frame order. To measure what this buys, we build SkelShift: four motion corpora under one codec, 143,334 captioned clips on skeletons of 4 to 142 joints, from which 4,232 clips on 2,043 skeletons are held out by clip, rig, tree class or species and organised along topology, joint count, body plan, species and rig source, and scored by motion quality: Fréchet distances on public kinetic features and on kinematic descriptors, foot skating and ground contact. Against UniMate trained on the same clips, on test rigs without a training clip, TopX lowers the descriptor FID from 5.73 to 4.58 when UniMate's guidance is tuned for the kinetic FID, and the kinetic FID from 1.48 to 0.55 when it is tuned for the descriptor FID, also on motions no training skeleton performs; fewer of its frames sink below the ground.

Learning Discriminative Prototypes via Boundary Repulsion MixUp for Object Re-Identification

Object re-identification requires embeddings that are robust to intra-class variation in viewpoint, illumination, and occlusion, yet preserve sharp separation between visually similar identities. The hardest errors arise precisely where confusable identities meet, a region that pairwise losses shape only implicitly. We present PBR-ReID, a prototype-based framework that makes this inter-class geometry an explicit learning target. Each identity is represented by a learnable prototype jointly optimized with a DiNOv3 backbone and the resulting prototype similarity matrix reveals, at every training step, which identities are most confusable. On this signal we build Boundary Repulsion MixUp (BRM), a geometry-aware regularizer that converts standard MixUp's weakness into a discriminative signal. Rather than interpolating samples across confusable identities, which blurs the boundaries that matter most, BRM constructs an explicit boundary proxy between each confusable pair and repels their features away from it, converting boundary ambiguity into a discriminative signal. We adopt a two-stage training protocol consisting of a prototype warmup phase followed by EMA-stabilized joint fine-tuning. Extensive experiments on person and vehicle ReID benchmarks demonstrate strong performance, with thorough ablations confirming each component's contribution. Code is available at https://github.com/psaltaath/pbr-reid.

Real-Time Conveyor Belt Defect Detection via Edge TPU-Accelerated YOLOv11 on Raspberry Pi 5

Conveyor belts are vital arteries in continuous bulk material handling, particularly in heavy process industries such as cement manufacturing. Undetected structural anomalies (longitudinal tears, holes, degraded patch repairs, and impact punctures) cause catastrophic belt snapping, costly downtime, and severe safety hazards. While deep learning offers high accuracy, existing models rely on power-hungry workstation GPUs, hindering practical deployment at remote industrial edge points. In this paper, we propose, implement, and validate an end-to-end, low-power edge AI vision system for real-time conveyor belt defect detection deployed on a Raspberry Pi 5 accelerated by a Google Coral Edge TPU coprocessor. The system leverages an optimized YOLOv11 nano (YOLOv11n) architecture trained on a multi-source industrial corpus exceeding 3,000 augmented samples from an operational cement plant in West Java, open-source datasets, and automated captures. The model undergoes post-training full integer quantization (INT8) compiled for systolic tensor execution. Experimental results demonstrate robust detection capability, achieving precision of 0.742, recall of 0.730, and mAP@0.5 of 0.764 across four defect classes. Deployed on-site in an IP-rated enclosure interfaced with an RTSP PoE industrial camera, the system achieves stable throughput of 30-40 FPS at 320x320 resolution with an end-to-end latency of 70-90 ms. Operational profiling under harsh clinker dust confirms thermal equilibrium at 55 +/- 5 deg C on the host CPU and ~65 deg C on the Edge TPU with zero frame loss. This demonstrates that edge AI bridges the gap between high-accuracy computer vision and cost-effective predictive maintenance in continuous manufacturing.

EditDistill: Is It Possible to Guide Video Editing with Image Editing?

Recent advances in image editing have enabled fine-grained and highly controllable visual modifications. However, achieving similar levels of controllability in video editing remains an open challenge, particularly for precise local edits, dynamic temporal effects, and consistent lighting across frames.In this paper, we propose EditDistill, a framework that distills image-level visual transformations from edits to guide video editing.Specifically, given a source video and an edit prompt, we first generate an edited version of the initial video frame using an off-the-shelf image editing model. We then distill the visual transformation of this original-edited image pair into a compact representation termed an edit descriptor.This descriptor is injected into a video diffusion model through our lightweight fusion module, which steers the denoising process toward the desired edit. To align the video trajectory with the demonstrated image edit while preserving motion and layout, we train with edit-descriptor alignment and DINO structural consistency losses, and derive an equivalent inference-time guidance formulation under flow matching.Extensive experiments demonstrate that our method achieves strong performance on standard benchmarks and produces high-quality video editing results across diverse scenarios. ChronoReplay: One Answer Interface Is Not Enough for Streaming Spatial Intelligence

Training a spatial VLM to answer which side an object lies on does not require it to preserve the metric displacement that determines that answer, while supervision for a distance estimate may leave categorical relations largely unconstrained. When post-training emphasizes one readout, improvements can therefore concentrate on that interface while transfer to others remains weak or even degrades; we call this observed specialization interface lock-in. ChronoReplay addresses it by replaying heterogeneous spatial supervision as chronologically ordered episodes, retaining source-native targets, and balancing supervision across families. Under the same observation access, a single ChronoReplay checkpoint improves OVO-S-Bench, OST-Bench, and UCS-Bench together, including a 13.41-point gain on OST-Bench, and achieves the highest three-benchmark mean among the supervision settings compared here. Same-data controls further separate the ingredients of this transfer: answer serialization redistributes gains across OST and UCS, family-balanced learning improves over ordinary joint SFT and natural turn weighting, and chronological replay exceeds a shifted-order control by about 2.41 OST points across two paired seeds. Adaptive frame selection then changes evidence access while keeping the trained model fixed, providing an additional inference-time gain at lower visual cost. Streaming spatial performance depends on what distinctions supervision preserves, how heterogeneous histories are organized during learning, and what causal evidence inference makes available at test time.

Robust Single-Parameter Point Cloud Registration using Truncated Lp Functions

We propose a novel method for robust point cloud registration with only one tunable parameter. Our method, dubbed TLP, uses truncated $L_p$ functions with different values of $p$ to assign priority scores to correspondences, select the hypothesis, and refine the final model. By leveraging the robustness of truncated functions, TLP effectively handles the high outlier rates commonly encountered in real-world 3D feature matching. We also exploit GPU parallelization to evaluate hypotheses efficiently, enabling registration from thousands of correspondences in under a second on average. With only one tunable parameter, our method offers greater practicality than multi-parameter methods that require dataset-specific tuning of several parameters. Evaluations on public benchmark datasets demonstrate that TLP achieves higher accuracy than state-of-the-art single-parameter methods. Furthermore, it outperforms two widely used multi-parameter methods on three of the four evaluated datasets while maintaining competitive runtime.

RacerGS: Residual-Autoregressive Context for 3D Gaussian Splatting Compression

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has emerged as a promising approach for fast, high-quality novel-view rendering. However, its large parameter size remains a bottleneck for storage and transmission. We propose RacerGS, a compact 3DGS compression framework that achieves scene reconstruction at significantly reduced storage sizes with preserved fidelity. To obtain compact and spatially coherent features, we design a hash projection model that projects the anchor hash encodings into compact context features. Additionally, we introduce a FiLM-modulated autoregressive GRU context model that utilizes causal dependencies among anchor feature groups and uses recurrent hidden states to refine entropy-model parameters, improving bitrate efficiency. Furthermore, we adaptively entropy-code quantized anchor-attribute residuals relative to their context-predicted means, leveraging spatial consistency to reduce symbol entropy. Overall, RacerGS achieves more than 150x compression over vanilla 3DGS and 28x over Scaffold-GS, while providing comparable rendering quality. Extensive experiments across five datasets demonstrate that RacerGS outperforms prior 3DGS compression methods in both storage reduction and rate-distortion performance.

$Δτ$-Bench: Pushing Agents Toward a Moving Capability

In this paper, we introduce $Δτ$-Bench, an executable agent benchmark that models the structural evolution of large tool ecosystems. In $Δτ$-Bench, independently evolving services continually reshape how tasks can be accomplished: familiar tools may disappear or be replaced, while more efficient alternatives emerge amid overlapping functionality and noisy documentation. Against this moving capability frontier, we evaluate whether agents can proactively discover efficient tool-use paths and promptly revise their accumulated procedural knowledge, bringing together demands that existing benchmarks fail to examine. $Δτ$-Bench comprises 1,588 validated multi-turn tasks across 42 domains and six successive ecosystem states generated through capability-preserving transformations. Our evaluation shows that even the strongest evaluated model or harness achieves only 42.3% task success, while initial gains from existing skill-learning methods can not consistently translate into better adaptation as the ecosystem evolves. These findings motivate Steward, an ecosystem manager that jointly manages tools and procedural knowledge through incremental skill maintenance and dynamic provisioning during task execution. Experiments show that Steward sustains higher task success across ecosystem changes with lower skill-maintenance overhead, while improving both recovery from disrupted workflows and adoption of more efficient alternatives.

ParetoGS: High-Quality Continuous-Budget 3D Gaussian Splatting with Sparse Adapted Anchors

Scalable 3D Gaussian Splatting allows one scene representation to serve different Gaussian-count budgets, yet its quality-budget curve may lag behind models optimized separately for each budget. Reducing the number of Gaussians changes both the selected members and the reconstruction tasks carried by the survivors; parameters inherited from a high-budget model may fit a low-budget representation poorly. We propose ParetoGS, which describes member changes and parameter evolution with a small set of adapted budget anchors and decodes intermediate budgets without separate optimization at every requested count. ParetoGS alternates pruning and fixed-member adaptation to build nested anchors with persistent identities, and encodes their parameter changes relative to the full model in a compact residual atlas. At decoding, it interpolates the states of shared members and gradually introduces the rendering contributions of new members, connecting sparse anchors into a continuous-budget representation. Across 13 scenes, ParetoGS attains better average PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS with fewer Gaussians than the compared prefix methods at 10% of each method's own full model. From a shared full model, it also outperforms FlexGS-imp at its trained 1%, 5%, and 10% budgets and NanoGS-imp at the 1% and 0.1% budgets. On Garden, its non-anchor quality approaches an empirical reference from independently optimized GNS models at higher retention ratios and exceeds it at some lower-budget points. Across 13 scenes, encoding reduces mean additional state from 390.0 to 85.7 MB with an average PSNR change of 0.01 dB. Project page: https://xiaobin2001.github.io/ParetoGS-Web/

BoundedGS: Memory-Efficient High-Resolution Training for 3D Gaussian Splatting

High-resolution 3D Gaussian Splatting training increases pixel and Gaussian-tile intersection counts, expanding rasterization caches that coexist with projection and gradient state at peak memory. A complete-view update aggregates gradients across the view; each region's forward and backward caches can be released after its contribution is collected. BoundedGS separates rasterization and projection backward passes and bounds the active local rasterization working set according to its measured load. Candidate-indexed projection and compact gradient storage address the view-level peaks exposed by this change, while CPU-assisted structure rebuilding and bounded image supply manage resources outside rendering. In a resolution sweep at a fixed count of about 6M Gaussians, the measured rendering working set stays within 1.073-1.077 GiB from 1K to 16K; at 11K, a warm pass takes 19.79 seconds versus 21.76 for full-frame execution. Across five native 5K outdoor scenes, full training reduces the mean rendering working set from 9.97 to 1.76 GiB (82.3%), retains similar reconstruction quality, and shortens training time from 36.7 to 33.9 minutes. CropReward: A Human-Preference Benchmark and Reward Model for Aesthetic Image Cropping

Aesthetic image cropping (AIC) aims to improve image composition and aesthetic quality by removing extraneous or distracting content. Over the past decade, AIC methods have evolved from specialized vision models to multimodal large language models(MLLMs). Reliable evaluation that reflects human preferences is essential for benchmarking AIC methods and guiding model optimization. However, the current AIC evaluation relies largely on geometry-based metrics such as intersection over union (IoU), as well as image aesthetic assessment(IAA) models. Although inconsistencies with human preferences have long been observed, the robustness and reliability of these evaluation criteria remain underexplored. To bridge this gap, we first construct CropBench, a large-scale benchmark comprising 106,995 human preference pairs for 1,019 images, with each candidate set containing the original image and candidate crops produced by 14 AIC methods. A systematic study on CropBench reveals that the existing criteria, ranging from IoU to off-the-shelf IAA assessment models, only exhibit limited alignment with human preferences. Building upon CropBench, we further propose CropReward, an MLLM-based reward model that evaluates a candidate crop by comparing with the original uncropped image and outputs a score indicating explicit aesthetic improvement or degradation relative to the original. In addition, we employ CropReward to construct CropReward-300k, the largest AIC dataset to date, and further develop CropAlign, a preference-aligned AIC model trained solely with CropReward supervision, without human-annotated crop boxes. Experimental results demonstrate that CropReward aligns with human preferences significantly better than existing criteria. CropAlign also surpasses the state-of-the-art AIC methods, demonstrating the advantage of CropReward in guiding AIC model optimization.

From Presence Labels to Bounding Boxes: Can Multimodal Large Language Models Scale Surgical Instrument Localization?

Using the best accuracy pipeline, we create and publicly release a spatial extension of Cholec80 comprising 285,977 bounding boxes across all 184,498 annotated frames from the 80 videos. Cross-modality medical image translation is challenged by anatomically inconsistent synthesis that can compromise clinical diagnosis. We propose StaX-Net, a Stackelberg interleaved asymmetric network that incorporates a partially coupled encoder-discriminator structure, and a 2D feature aggregation and spatial transformation (FAST) layer that distills target-supervised priors under the learning using privileged information paradigm. This constrains the synthesis process through target-informed feature representations relative to standard generative architectures. The code is available on GitHub for public use.

Moreover, counterfactual supervision improves control in several settings where standard supervision is weaker. Finally, we test our method on real-world news video and find that visual control improves when the relevant evidence is made easier to access. Overall, our results show that reliable modality control requires testing not only whether an answer is correct, but whether it continues to follow edits to the modality of choice.
