GazeRIB: Systematic Evaluation of Multi-Reader Gaze-Guided Chest X-ray Learning

Radiologists' visual attention can supervise medical image learning, but how reader panel composition affects its value remains untested. We evaluated GazeRIB (Gaze-guided Radiologist-Informed Blackout), which masks unviewed regions during training and uses unmasked images at inference. Eight experts repeatedly read the same 80 chest X-rays (CXRs). We compared individual readers with three-, five- and eight-reader panels across seven architectures and 10 seeds, evaluating 910 models on a separate 2,897-CXR cohort for four-class severity prediction. Every panel improved macro F1 over the no-gaze baseline by 5.33-7.47 percentage points (pp; Bonferroni-adjusted p < 0.007); no individual reader reached significance on this endpoint. Three readers captured 73-80% of the eight-reader gain in macro F1, the area under the receiver-operating-characteristic curve (ROC-AUC) and the area under the precision-recall curve (AU-PRC). On external validation without retraining, all four panels improved ROC-AUC on SIIM-FISABIO-RSNA by +1.93 to +4.24 pp (p <= 0.004); on MIMIC-Eye no panel differed significantly from baseline, whereas the single-reader average declined (-1.40 pp, p = 0.008). Reader composition therefore influenced performance, although transfer depended on the cohort.

Latent Reasoning in Continuous Space for Unified Multimodal Models

Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) achieve remarkable success on diverse multimodal understanding and generation tasks, yet they still struggle to effectively leverage language-based chain-of-thought reasoning for image generation. As a result, existing reasoning-augmented UMMs often fail to inherit the test-time scaling and fine-grained controllability observed in large language models. We attribute this failure to a fundamental modality mismatch: reasoning is performed in a discrete token space, whereas image generation operates in a continuous space. To bridge this gap, we introduce a Latent Reasoning framework in Continuous space (LARC), which alleviates the token-level bottleneck by reasoning over continuous hidden states. Specifically, the training framework consists of two stages: (i) curriculum supervised fine-tuning (SFT), and (ii) information-gain reinforcement learning (RL). First, the curriculum SFT stage gradually converts token-level reasoning into latent reasoning. Subsequently, the information-gain RL stage self-evolves latent reasoning without ground truth supervision. Experiments across diverse text-to-image generation benchmarks show that LARC consistently improves generation quality, prompt following, and compositional fidelity. Notably, LARC achieves state-of-the-art performance on GenEval with a 7%p gain over BAGEL and demonstrates clear test-time scaling behavior, outperforming token-based generation under both parallel and sequential test-time scaling strategies.

SemEllipsoid: Semantic-aware Shape Abstraction with Ellipsoid

Representing 3D shapes using a set of primitives - known as shape abstraction - is an actively studied problem in computer graphics with wide applications in shape understanding, analysis, and simulation due to the compactness and computational efficiency of the primitive-based representation. Prior work has primarily focused on geometry-aware shape abstraction, where the objective is to approximate the input shape with a minimal set of primitives while preserving geometric fidelity. However, these methods often overlook semantic consistency, inevitably leading to primitives that span across multiple semantic parts. Such inconsistency can cause undesirable artifacts in downstream tasks such as part-level shape editing or manipulation. In this paper, we introduce a new task - semantic-aware shape abstraction, where the goal is to produce abstractions that are both geometrically faithful and semantically consistent, ensuring that each primitive adheres strictly to a single semantic part without crossing semantic boundaries. We propose SemEllipsoid, a novel method to address this problem using ellipsoidal primitives. The core idea is to inject semantic constraints into the primitive optimization process leveraging the availability of semantic cues from large-scale foundation models. We design a fully differentiable projection-blending-evaluation scheme to measure semantic divergence against input semantic masks, and formulate an alternating optimization strategy to iteratively solve for optimal semantic assignment and primitive configurations. Our method is non-learning-based, requiring no data collection or network training, and generalizes well to shapes from arbitrary categories. It also supports different levels of abstraction according to user demand. Extensive experiments and evaluations validate the effectiveness of our approach across several 3D shape collections.

RoboArchitect: Automating the Design of Robotic Agents with Persistent Memory

Recent work shows that a frozen robot policy performs far better inside an agent harness, where control code decides when to query the policy, consult vision tools, or command the arm directly. Yet today these harnesses are built by hand and redesigned for each new robot or benchmark. We show that harness design can be automated, offering a strong alternative to finetuning on a novel setup. In RoboArchitect, a coding model observes a robot's rollouts, diagnoses failures, and iteratively revises the harness around the frozen policy. The harness evolves on a set of episodes for its optimization and is evaluated on held-out scenes and tasks. Without updating any model weights, RoboArchitect raises average success from 9.7% to 43.3% on unseen scenes (4.5x) and from 12.7% to 40.2% on unseen tasks (3.2x), across multiple benchmarks and policies. The gains are largest where the benchmark is far from the policy's training data: wrapping a general-purpose policy, the evolved harness yields a specialist that often outperforms even the finetuned version. Intuitive control strategies are automatically designed, such as lifting a jammed arm clear or reaching in horizontally when an obstacle blocks the way from above. The harness also evolves its own persistent memory, carrying learned experience, such as effective grasp depths, into later decisions. These results suggest that substantial headroom in robotic manipulation lies in the agent harness, and its design can be effectively automated and optimized without weight updates.

ReLift: Dense 3D Tracking as Lifted Trajectory Repair

Dense 3D tracking from monocular video requires establishing point correspondences over time, estimating their depth, and inferring their trajectories during occlusion. Existing methods typically learn these tasks jointly, a demanding objective given the scarcity of 4D supervision. We show that strong pretrained 2D trackers and 3D geometry models provide an effective foundation: lifted trajectories, with occluded segments simply interpolated, already rival dedicated 3D trackers using the same geometry. However, lifted trajectories inherit correspondence and depth errors, and under occlusion, their depth comes from the occluder rather than the tracked point. We introduce ReLift, an efficient feed-forward framework that recasts dense 3D tracking as geometric trajectory repair and completion. At its core, a trajectory corrector alternates attention along and across trajectories to preserve reliable observations, repair corrupted positions, and complete missing motion. Using only geometric inputs allows us to train directly on 3D trajectories with simulated lifting errors, decoupling training from images and from any specific tracker or geometry model. Concentrating 4D supervision on trajectory refinement yields a reusable model that consistently improves lifted trajectories across trackers and geometry providers without retraining, so it benefits directly from future advances in either component. On WorldTrack, ReLift achieves state-of-the-art mean accuracy, improving on the strongest baselines by 2.1 APD on all points and 3.0 on dynamic points, with faster configurations remaining competitive with leading 3D trackers while running faster than 4RC. A Better Critic Matters for Distribution Matching Distillation

Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) trains a few-step student using the difference between a teacher's score and an online critic's estimate of the student distribution's score. We investigate what the critic needs, in capacity and in information, to provide a reliable training signal. On the capacity side, controlled experiments across seven SiT model sizes on ImageNet show that increasing teacher size while keeping a small critic fixed can degrade student quality and cause training collapse. Scaling the critic alongside the teacher, a strategy we call joint scaling, prevents collapse in our experiments and allows the student to benefit from larger teachers. On the information side, rather than forcing the critic to model a step-agnostic mixture of student outputs, we introduce a step-aware critic (STAC) that conditions on the student's generation step to estimate step-specific scores. Together, joint scaling and STAC consistently improve few-step distillation across class-conditional ImageNet generation, text-to-image generation with SDXL and FLUX.2, and text-to-video generation with Wan2.1.

Rethinking Asymmetry in Self-Supervised 3D Geometry Learning

Self-supervised learning relies on asymmetry between what a model observes and what it must explain. Recent methods learn 3D geometry from unposed images by predicting cameras and scene structure, then rendering held-out views. To avoid trivial solutions, they impose this asymmetry in the input space, restricting how views interact through separate branches or masked attention. We revisit this design with geometric accuracy, rather than rendering quality, as the main objective. When a held-out view lies between context views, the context already constrains the geometry it observes. We therefore impose the asymmetry in the 3D representation space, excluding held-out views only from the reconstructed scene while a shared backbone uses all views for correspondence. This improves depth and pose estimation over input-space designs. We further exploit view density: sparse and dense observations depict the same scene but provide different amounts of correspondence. Aligning sparse-context features with dense-context features supplies a geometric training signal alongside rendering, without a pretrained geometric teacher. We additionally align features with a frozen vision foundation model, which brings further gains. None of these signals requires geometric annotation, and the resulting model improves geometry estimation over prior self-supervised methods. Controlled experiments isolate the contribution of each component, and self-supervised pretraining also improves subsequent supervised training.

Think in 360°: Benchmarking and Bootstrapping Panoramic Video Reasoning

A panoramic video captures all directions around the camera, but relevant evidence can appear in different directions and at different times. Understanding it requires models to connect observations across the viewing sphere and time. We introduce PanoVideoBench, the first manually annotated benchmark for comprehensive panoramic video understanding. Its 1,000 questions span five categories and 15 question types, with diagnostic annotations for target search and panoramic capabilities. Evaluation of 11 proprietary, open-source, and panoramic-specialized models identifies cross-view tracking and sphere-time evidence integration as their weakest capabilities. To strengthen these capabilities, we construct PanoVideoCoT, the first reasoning dataset grounded in real panoramic video, through sphere-time-grounded synthesis that converts verified panoramic observations and successful agentic explorations into 13,465 QA-CoT pairs. Starting from a general video MLLM, PanoVideoReasoner learns from this corpus through Panoramic CoT SFT and Evidence-Privileged On-Policy Self-Distillation, a panoramic instantiation of Vision-OPD's OPSD in which the teacher uses verified perspective views to guide student-generated trajectories. It reaches 55.7% on PanoVideoBench, improving the backbone by 12.5 points and the previous SOTA by 2.6. The gains concentrate on the same spatial, cross-view, and temporal capabilities exposed by the benchmark, showing that grounded sphere-time supervision can equip a general video MLLM with panoramic reasoning.

REA: A Routed Embodied Agent Runtime for Asynchronous Brain-Cerebellum Control

Foundation models are becoming remarkably reliable at high-level reasoning, yet they remain too slow and costly to serve as fine-grained controllers. Conversely, fast policies like VLAs and whole-body controllers offer more efficient reflexes, but struggle to generalize. While pairing a deliberative brain (foundation models) with a reactive cerebellum (fast policies) seems natural for embodied systems, two obstacles remain: methods are built and scored on incompatible stacks, and their asynchronous coordination is difficult. For example, an agent that calls the VLA in the agentic loop can stall the fast execution, while a VLA that calls the agent on errors tends to re-trigger fallbacks repeatedly or completely miss silent failures. To address this, we propose REA (Routed Embodied Agent), a unified runtime where a deliberative System 2, an embodied router System 1 and reactive System 0 policies cooperate under a single golden rule - no running policy waits on the brain. Instead of querying the brain at each failure, REA employs a route memory that stores each policy's measured success ahead of time, a boundary-triggered judge gate that consults the brain on policy execution, and a shared-budget escalation ladder for bounded recovery. Evaluated on 3,000 test episodes across 60 LIBERO-Pro tasks, REA reaches 95.9% success and beats the best single-agent baseline in its own policy pool (94.7%) while reducing control steps by 25% and control time by 17%. In a separate 100-episode RoboTwin 2.0 study, REA achieves 84% success with VLA policies and 86% with VLA+code, compared with 80% for the strongest single VLA baseline MEDex. These results demonstrate learned and programmatic policies operating within the same runtime. REA also transfers to BEHAVIOR-1K and a real Unitree G1 humanoid, achieving the highest observed success among the compared methods.

Learning Clean Generative Processes from Noisy Measurements

We study how to learn a generative model of clean images using only noisy observations. Diffusion processes and their generalizations to non-Gaussian noise require posterior predictions at every noise level. We first show that it is possible to learn these predictions from independent noisy views using Noise2Noise regression, but only at or above the noise level of the training data. To learn the predictions below that level, needed to generate clean images, we impose consistency: a prediction should equal its expectation after continuing the sampling process. We derive conditions under which consistency uniquely determines the continuation to the clean image distribution and verify them for Poisson, binomial, Gamma, and negative-binomial measurements. Experiments on CIFAR-10, photon-counting microscopy, and synthetic aperture radar show that the required posterior predictions can be learned from noisy views alone and that the consistency relation repairs the continuation beyond the noise level of the data, which severely deteriorates without consistency.

Any Forcing: Any-Step Generality and One-Step Stability for Autoregressive Video Generation

Autoregressive video generation enables efficient inference through streaming prediction. However, existing methods are typically restricted to fixed sampling steps, limiting the flexibility to balance generation quality and efficiency, while one-step generation suffers from degraded visual quality and error accumulation over long sequences, leading to content drift and generation collapse. To address these issues, we propose Any Forcing, a unified framework for achieving Any-Step Generality and One-Step Stability. Specifically, we introduce a unified adversarial discriminator that jointly performs real-distribution alignment, inter-step consistency alignment, and first-frame quality enhancement, enabling flexible sampling while preserving one-step generation quality. Furthermore, we propose a training-free adaptive long-term memory selection mechanism that selects historical states based on global semantic relevance and local value contribution, while remapping their temporal positions to preserve temporal relationships within the memory cache, thereby enabling stable one-step long-video generation. Experiments demonstrate that Any Forcing achieves high-quality and stable one-step autoregressive video generation, achieving 20.9 FPS and 24.7 FPS for short and long video generation on a single H100 GPU, respectively.

GeoFlip: Benchmarking Geometry-Sensitive Physics in Video Generation

Video generation models synthesize realistic motion, but can they respond correctly to changes in initial geometry? We introduce GeoFlip, a video generation benchmark centered on physical outcome flips induced by geometry. With shared physical settings and textual prompts held fixed, GeoFlip applies controlled interventions to support extent, object placement, and mechanism configuration, constructing paired scenes with contrasting reference events, such as remaining stable versus falling or tipping. These pairs test whether models translate geometric differences in the first frame into correct physical evolution. The benchmark comprises 482 scene pairs and 964 reference videos across six physical categories, with reference outcomes established through procedural scene construction, physics simulation, and numerical validation and verification of physical events. We evaluate 16 models for image to video generation along three dimensions: event correctness, structural integrity, and similarity to reference videos. We examine evaluation consistency with independent vision language judges. On the 442 conditions shared by all models, the highest event correctness averaged across tasks is 27.98%, revealing substantial limitations in physical generation conditioned on geometry. Model strengths vary across physical mechanisms, and the three evaluation dimensions capture distinct capabilities. Typical failures include ignoring geometric changes, generating incorrect object motion, bending rigid objects, and introducing additional components. Rank correlations between judges reach 0.906 for event correctness and 0.929 for structural integrity. By combining controlled geometric interventions, verifiable physical references, and multidimensional evaluation, GeoFlip assesses progress from visual realism toward physical consistency conditioned on geometry.

MoETok: Sparse Expert Routing for 1D Visual Tokenizers

Modern visual tokenizers aim to preserve fine-grained visual details while learning high-level semantic representations for downstream generation and understanding. Query-based 1D visual tokenizers encode images into flexible latent sequences, yet how individual learnable queries gather and encode image information remains poorly understood. We reveal that these queries spontaneously develop complementary roles under semantic supervision: a small set of Content queries captures high-level semantic information, while a larger set of Detail queries preserves fine-grained visual details for reconstruction. To adapt token processing to these heterogeneous roles, we introduce MoETok, a 1D visual tokenizer that routes tokens through sparsely activated experts. Jointly optimized with reconstruction and semantic objectives, the router learns role-specialized processing paths adapted to each token's representation. Across model scales and token budgets, MoETok consistently improves reconstruction, semantic representation, and autoregressive generation over dense tokenizers under matched supervision and active computation. On ImageNet, it combines strong reconstruction and semantic representation with state-of-the-art class-conditional generation within the 1D tokenizer family. We further validate MoETok at scale in a unified autoregressive model jointly trained for understanding and generation with a next-token cross-entropy objective. The resulting model combines competitive visual understanding with state-of-the-art generation among unified discrete models, matching or surpassing diffusion-based models such as BAGEL and Qwen-Image on benchmarks including DPG-Bench (87.61), GenEval2 (40.04 GM), and WISE (0.49).

FoAViS: Analyzing Learned Fixation Strategies in a Foveated Active Vision System

Human vision is foveated and active: resolution is highest at the fovea and falls off towards the periphery, and fixations are actively directed to locations of interest. Building a machine system that processes information in the same way lets us study gaze behavior in a controlled setting where the system's components and objectives are known. The emergence of gaze characteristics can therefore be examined systematically, which may in turn inform our understanding of biological vision. This requires a model that can sample like the fovea, handle various tasks from classification to dense prediction, and be trained concurrently with a fixation policy. In our study, we present such a system and study its capabilities and the fixation policies that emerge from it. Our system, FoAViS, samples visual input by adopting the established log-polar foveation and accumulates information in a 2D spatial memory. Due to its 2D structure, the same memory can be read out globally for classification or densely for semantic segmentation, without any architectural change. Fixations are drawn from a 2D map by a policy trained concurrently with the task. We find that learned fixation policies select more informative, task-specific locations than non-learned heuristics. These policies also shape the task representations in a beneficial way: for both classification and segmentation, models trained concurrently with a policy outperform models trained with random locations, even when the latter are provided with learned scanpaths. Moreover, these scanpaths exhibit similarities to human gaze behavior by capturing aspects of empirical scanpath characteristics. Our work marks an important step away from standard passive, single-pass architectures, towards biologically grounded models of vision. It opens a path for studying goal-directed gaze behavior and information representation in the visual system.

TwinPast: Benchmarking Historical Evidence Use in Streaming Video Understanding with Evidence Contrastive Video Pairs

High accuracy on streaming video benchmarks does not establish whether a model uses visual history. Our review of OVO-Bench and StreamingBench finds that recent frames suffice for 32% of sampled historical questions, while models also answer many questions correctly from the question and options alone. We introduce TwinPast to test whether answers correctly follow changes in history, using 350 human-reviewed video pairs spanning five historical tasks. Each pair holds the recent video context, question, and ordered options fixed while changing critical historical evidence and the correct answer. Each video is evaluated independently, and PairAcc requires correct answers for both histories. We evaluate 34 model and input configurations. For Qwen3-VL-8B and DeepSeek-Flash, historical inputs improve performance on TwinPast, reversing the recent-frame advantage on existing benchmarks. The strongest evaluated streaming system reaches 49.14% PairAcc, compared with 66.57-91.43% for the evaluated general-purpose models. This gap persists across all five tasks. Case studies further show that models select states from the wrong time or contradict recorded event order despite retaining the relevant evidence. These findings highlight the need to connect historical memory and retrieval with reasoning that uses evidence according to the question's temporal references and event relations.

IGDA-ICMH: an Importance Guided Feature and Network Dual Adaptation Framework towards Image Compression for Machine and Human Vision

With the surge of machine vision applications, image compression for both machine and human vision (ICMH) has become an important research area. Existing studies mainly focus on task-specific adaptation or multi-task joint optimization, yet they often overlook the unequal contributions of different features and network layers to downstream machine vision tasks. To address this issue, we first quantitatively investigate the impact of different feature and network layers on machine vision performance, providing a principled understanding of their importance. Based on this analysis, an Importance Guided feature and network Dual Adaptation (IGDA) framework is developed for ICMH. It adopts implicit neural representation as the basic architecture and designs an efficient adaptation structure with both feature and network weight adaptation. An Importance-Guided Network Adaptation (IGNA) module is developed with a low-rank adaptation strategy to selectively adjust key network layers according to their importance in different tasks. An Importance-Guided Feature Adaptation (IGFA) module is developed with a low-rank and Kronecker product-based transform to explore the non-local property of the features in order to reduce the number of adaptation parameters. Extensive experiments on representative machine vision tasks, including image classification and object detection, demonstrate that our IGDA-ICMH framework achieves significantly better performance than the existing implicit neural representation-based methods for ICMH.

Movens-Zero: Scaling Humanoid Motion Foundation Models with Large-Scale Human Videos

Scaling humanoid motion foundation models is constrained by the scarcity of diverse, robot-executable data and by the difficulty of jointly learning from heterogeneous multimodal inputs while supporting real-time closed-loop control. We present Movens-5M, constructed from raw Internet videos through clip generation, quality filtering, semantic annotation, motion recovery, and physical refinement with humanoid retargeting and execution verification. It contains 15 million human-motion sequences and 5 million physically executable humanoid-motion sequences spanning over 10,000 hours, aligned with text, RGB video, speech, and music. Building on this data, we introduce Movens-Zero, a scalable understanding-generation-execution architecture that supports joint learning across modalities and real-time closed-loop execution. Task-mixed flow-matching training jointly optimizes a single action expert across seven generation tasks, enabling direct motion generation from text, speech, music, and video. Multimodal Real-Time Chunking connects overlapping chunks under continuously updated instructions and robot states for continuous closed-loop execution. We further introduce Movens-Intent, a four-track benchmark with 8,192 expert-verified condition-motion pairs across text, speech, music, and video. Experiments show consistent gains across all four modalities when scaling execution-verified data from 1% to 100% and action-expert capacity from 64M to 911M parameters, while demonstrating that data quality and cross-modal supervision improve generation and execution.

Open-Weight MLLMs Cannot Compare What They Cannot Name

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are increasingly capable of understanding images and performing complex visual reasoning, suggesting the possibility of using them to automate biological image analysis. This need is becoming increasingly urgent as advances in technology continue to generate ever larger and more diverse microscopy datasets, already exceeding the scale at which manual visual analysis is feasible. With the final aim to use MLLMs for visual comparison of complex phenotypes, we investigate a very simple baseline: comparison of the size of body parts in synthetic images of ants. We find that state-of-the-art open-weight MLLMs fail for some body parts, but not for others, even though the comparison itself is visually trivial for all of them. We evaluate different in-context learning strategies and analyze performance on the related object recognition task. Our results suggest a recognition-driven grounding failure: structures that the models cannot recognize are instead associated with a recognizable semantic concept, impairing the grounding and comparison of both. Further analysis of the vision encoder is consistent with the hypothesis that fine-grained visual structure may be less well preserved without semantic support. Together, our results reveal a surprising limitation of current MLLMs: semantic recognition can gate simple visual comparisons that do not intrinsically require semantic understanding. ReuScope: Auditing Data Reuse and Evaluation Sensitivity in Capsule Vision 2024

We develop ReuScope, a protocol for auditing data reuse and evaluation sensitivity, using Capsule Vision 2024 as a case study. It combines measurements of data relationships with two comparisons: changing the evaluation population while holding predictions fixed, and changing training data while holding evaluation fixed. Byte-identical files cross the public training-validation boundary, and all 41 Kvasir-Capsule recordings that contribute frames occur in both partitions. Shared source recordings establish cross-split overlap; missing cross-recording patient identifiers prevent counting unique shared patients. Across ten paired seeds, removing all training rows attributed to Kvasir-Capsule, plus five rows from other sources flagged as near-matches to it, lowers public-validation balanced accuracy from 82.5% to 61.2%. Evaluating the same fitted models on the validation rows of the other sources reverses the comparison: removal improves balanced accuracy by 1.38 percentage points. The public-validation drop measures sensitivity to source removal, not an amount of leakage-induced inflation. Matched controls show that sample size and class counts alone do not explain the observed score differences. An exploratory experiment removes the training neighbors of validation frames that have close training matches, or equally many distant training rows, and yields a difference-in-differences estimate, relative to matched unflagged frames, of -0.355 percentage points in true-class probability; the paired-t interval for accuracy spans zero. The resulting procedure distinguishes observed reuse from conditional performance sensitivity and specifies the population, class support and uncertainty unit for each conclusion.

PGGT-Loc: Primitive-Grounded Geometry Transformer for Feed-Forward Camera Localization

Camera localization methods are mostly multi-stage pipelines relying on dedicated and complex maps that are costly to construct, store, and maintain over time. Alternatively, well-structured maps that organize scenes with compact yet expressive primitives are often readily available, providing rich structural and semantic cues that humans can interpret. However, their potential for direct camera localization remains largely unexplored due to the significant modality gap between map primitives and visual observations. This motivates a new concept for localization, which estimates camera poses by grounding query observations to map primitives. In this paper, we instantiate the concept in the context of planar primitives with PGGT, the Primitive-Grounded Geometry Transformer network. Given a single query image and a set of planar map primitives, the network is trained to predict multiple localization targets for feed-forward 6-DoF camera localization that generalizes to unseen environments. With this straightforward and scalable architecture, our method pushes the state-of-the-art localization performance on both dense surface maps and minimal layout models. A Patch is More Than a Token

Vision Transformers represent each image patch through a canonical token produced under one observation condition. That token is a powerful interface, but it is still one realized state of the patch. We introduce PMTT, which establishes two foundations: a patch representation object can extend from one canonical state to a canonical-centered Representation State Family, and identity-preserving variation opens Patch-Level Local Feature Space. We develop four parts of this representation program. Patch Information Acquisition and Dense Orbital Views expose lawful same-patch states. Visible-to-Hidden prediction tests whether observed states reveal structure beyond themselves. Intrinsic analysis identifies low-dimensional, energy-concentrated, patch-specific, predictive geometry. Representation Organization separates acquiring more information from organizing that information, with an analytic Manifold as the central structured representation. We realize PMTT on frozen DINOv3 S/B/L/H+ backbones without neural post-training of the representation. Across two independent 4,096-anchor cohorts, Manifold explains 70.8-79.6% of Hidden variation, compared with 55.0-72.3% for directly Observed states, and wins on more than 95% of anchors at every scale. Effective rank falls from about 2.5 to 2.0 while dominant energy concentration rises with backbone scale. Across six visual capabilities and four scales, Observed exceeds the canonical Center in 21 of 24 conditions, Manifold exceeds Center in 23, and Manifold exceeds Observed in 16.
