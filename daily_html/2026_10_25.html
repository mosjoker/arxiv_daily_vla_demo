Grounded Exploration for Open-Ended Image Editing

Open-ended image-editing requests, such as ``decorate this room,'' admit multiple valid realizations. Direct sampling may produce ungrounded variation, whereas explicit diversity prompting often produces superficial differences. We study how to ground these requests without prematurely eliminating their legitimate ambiguity. We introduce a two-stage framework that first identifies diverse, feasible edit scopes and then realizes them as executable edit instructions. Across 300 open-ended image-editing tasks, our approach achieves 24.6% higher mean semantic dispersion and 17.1% higher mean groundedness than direct diversity prompting, while producing more valid and distinct realizations. These results show that structuring feasible edit directions before realization supports more useful grounded exploration.

ProMo3D: Probing Motion Cues in Frozen 3D Foundation Models

Feed-forward 3D foundation models have recently reshaped multi-view reconstruction, yet dynamic 4D understanding still often relies on large-scale motion supervision or hand-crafted, architecture-specific heuristics. We ask whether frozen 3D foundation model tokens already encode motion-relevant information that can be exposed by lightweight readout functions, rather than re-learning motion-specific features with high-capacity decoders. Through probing on three representative backbones, we find that static--dynamic separation is decodable from frozen token representations. Our rank-restricted and spectral analyses on $π^3$X reveal that the recovered motion cues are concentrated in a low-dimensional subspace. Building on this, we propose Probing-based Motion Distillation (PMD), which converts pairwise motion-state compatibility scores against a fixed anchor token into dense motion masks. Trained on only 160 YouTube-VOS videos using a single 16 GB GPU, PMD transfers zero-shot to DAVIS, SegTrackv2, and FBMS-59 without target-benchmark fine-tuning. With multi-view pretrained geometric backbones, PMD outperforms training-free heuristics and approaches substantially heavier supervised and self-supervised methods, suggesting that motion decoding from frozen 3D foundation models can be lightweight and data-efficient.

Whispers from the Neighbors: Rethinking Caption Consensus as Inference-Time Evidence for Vision-Language Retrieval

An image is rarely described only once. Retrieval benchmarks and many collections attach several captions to each picture, yet dual encoders still judge every image-caption pair in isolation. We ask what these neighboring captions contribute when a frozen encoder consults them at inference time. Fusing image similarity with similarity to an image's own captions improves text-to-image R@1 by 3.4 to 16.8 points across nine frozen encoders when the captions come from the same annotators as the queries. The gain falls to 1.9 to 7.2 points when an off-the-shelf captioner writes them, and to at most one point when they come from a different annotation source; a paraphrase-trained text encoder strengthens the text side further. A natural refinement, scoring the part of the image that the captions leave unexplained, appears to add much more under a leave-one-out evaluation. We show that this advantage arises because the correct candidate's supports are built differently from those of the negative candidates, with set size and query removal each contributing about half, and that under a fixed support index the residual reduces to a reweighting of what the captions already provide. On compositional benchmarks, the captions help by matching candidates to human descriptions of the image, without improving image-text grounding. One Scan is Enough: Demonstration-Free Adaptation for Language-Guided Navigation

Deploying a language-guided navigation policy in a new building usually means watching it fail. Public-benchmark VLAs degrade scene by scene as layout, appearance, and language drift, and the standard fix of teleoperating fresh trajectories on site does not scale. We replace human demonstration with a single brief iPhone scan. Scan2Instruction compiles that scan into a complete training environment, automatically synthesizing executable navigation episodes with grounded instructions. PRPT-KS then adapts the policy through a closed-form Pareto utility read directly off the reconstructed scene, with no learned critic and no human labels. Across five real environments, the two designs together drive our RealNav to an 85% real-robot success rate, compared with 49% for a RealNav baseline post-trained on human demonstrations, and still achieve 82% in unscanned regions of the same buildings, compared with 41% for the same baseline. Our work turns demonstration-free scene adaptation for language-guided navigation from an aspiration into a working reality.

Segmentation of Moving Objects During Camera Motion Using the OWL Function

We present a principled, analytical framework for segmenting multiple independently moving rigid objects during single camera motion, built directly upon the OWL perception function. OWL encodes two fundamental visual motion cues, perceived looming $L$ and perceived rotation $ω$, into a single closed-form representation of relative 3D structure. A key consequence of this structure is that the unit direction of relative translation $\hat{t}$ is analytically recoverable per image point from $L$ and $ω$ alone. All points belonging to a non-rotating rigid body share the same $\hat{t}$, whereas points on different rigid bodies generally yield distinct values. This furnishes a natural segmentation criterion: clustering per-point $\hat{t}$ estimates partitions the visible scene into independently moving rigid bodies. Building on the 2D complex form of OWL, we derive the framework in 3D using quaternions, for a single moving camera. The approach is parallel, camera rotation-invariant, and requires no training data, no depth information, and no semantic priors.

RoboReason: Boosting Robotics-Relevant VLM Reasoning Through Scalable Data Generation

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have demonstrated impressive performance when integrated into robot manipulation pipelines, but real-world evaluation of VLM capabilities on downstream robotics tasks is both difficult and expensive to scale. We propose that spatial and temporal ordering tasks using images from stereo and wrist cameras attached to real-world robots are particularly well-suited for evaluating visual reasoning capabilities, as they assess skills directly applicable to robots. We evaluate VLM reasoning abilities on three questions: ordering shuffled frames conditioned on a language description of the task, ordering objects depthwise based on distance from the camera, and ordering objects from left-to-right in pixel coordinates. The complexity of these tasks can also be modulated by changing the size of the set to sort. Our experiments show that several state-of-the-art vision-language models struggle on ordering tasks. To fix this, we propose a scalable autonomous training data generation pipeline, and show that both supervised finetuning and reinforcement-learning on the generated data can significantly improve VLM reasoning performance both on spatiotemporal ordering tasks as well as downstream robotics and visual reasoning tasks.

C-DFF: Concept-Level Diagnostics for Mechanism Parity in Simulation-Based Validation of Autonomous Driving Perception

Simulation is widely used in autonomous-driving validation, but matching task outputs between real and synthetic images does not guarantee that a system relies on the same visual evidence. This creates a validation gap for transfer, especially in safety-critical perception functions. We present Concept-Decisive Feature Fidelity (C-DFF), a concept-level mechanism-parity diagnostic for paired real-synthetic evaluation. Starting from decisive evidence extracted from a frozen system-under-test (SUT), C-DFF projects that evidence into a shared semantic concept space using off-the-shelf segmentation and compares real and synthetic concept-importance vectors. The result is not only a scalar mechanism-parity score, but also a concept-shift diagnostic that identifies which scene concepts become more or less decisive in simulation. We evaluate C-DFF on KITTI $\leftrightarrow$ Virtual KITTI 2 with 2126 matched pairs across three frozen SUTs (steering regression, YOLOP drivable-area, YOLOP lane-line). Across these tasks, C-DFF is SUT-discriminative and yields interpretable concept-level summaries that support validation triage and simulator debugging. When reported alongside task-specific output-value context metrics, C-DFF shows that output agreement does not by itself establish mechanism parity. These results position C-DFF as a practical semantic diagnostic layer for validation and verification workflows in simulation-based AV testing.

Road Infrastructure Perception for Autonomous Driving: Taxonomy, Representations, Tasks, and Challenges

Road infrastructure perception is a fundamental component of autonomous driving, as it recovers the structured road elements that define roadway geometry, delimit operational space, and encode traffic regulations. Although individual tasks such as lane detection, drivable-space estimation, traffic-sign recognition, and online map construction have been extensively studied, the field remains fragmented across task definitions, sensing assumptions, output representations, datasets, and evaluation protocols. This survey provides a function-oriented synthesis of road infrastructure perception. We first clarify its scope and organize existing studies into three complementary categories: geometric-structural, functional-spatial, and regulatory-semantic infrastructure. Based on this taxonomy, we review sensing modalities, scene representations, representative methods, benchmarks, and evaluation protocols, emphasizing the field's evolution from image-space recognition toward metric, vectorized, topology-aware, occupancy-based, and rule-grounded scene modeling. We further examine how representation choices affect evaluation and downstream usability, revealing limitations of current benchmarks in relation reasoning, temporal consistency, robustness, and planning-oriented assessment. Finally, we discuss open challenges and future directions toward unified infrastructure representation, open-environment generalization, relation- and rule-aware reasoning, and downstream-oriented evaluation. By connecting tasks, representations, and evaluation under a common functional framework, this survey aims to establish road infrastructure perception as a coherent foundation for map-compatible and behavior-relevant autonomous-driving scene understanding.

Computer Vision Navigation for an Autonomous Vehicle Model Using Reinforcement Learning

This work aims to assess the capabilities of the reinforcement learning algorithm, Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), in developing a policy capable of controlling the speed and steering angle of a vehicle model to navigate within simulated road lines. The control system's input is a camera, and its outputs are the rotational speed of the rear wheels and the steering angle of the front wheels, controlling the vehicle's speed and direction, respectively. The algorithm is trained and tested in a simulated environment, where it receives image frames from the vehicle's camera, processes them through a feature extraction algorithm, and feeds them into the policy. For each frame, the policy outputs speed and direction commands for the vehicle. The policy is iteratively updated to enhance its performance. The reinforcement learning algorithm successfully generates a policy capable of navigating the vehicle in a simplified environment. Potential improvements are suggested to enhance the system's robustness in more complex scenarios. Creating a simulation environment suitable for testing an advanced reinforcement learning algorithm is a complex task. This project offers a framework for other developers to test different approaches and algorithms. The tested algorithm, PPO, uses proximity intervals - an innovative approach in reinforcement learning. In some scenarios, the algorithm achieved better performance than predecessor algorithms, which are typically harder to implement and require tuning more hyperparameters.

A SegFormer-based model for spatial Daylight Factor prediction with comprehensive obstruction encoding and regulatory validation

Daylight compliance assessment in residential buildings requires time-consuming raytracing simulations and specialist model preparation, limiting its integration into iterative architectural design. We present a machine learning-based methodology for predicting the spatial distribution of the Daylight Factor (DF) directly within Building Information Modelling (BIM) environments, delivering results in seconds without model export or manual curation. The approach addresses five gaps not jointly covered by any existing model: external obstruction encoding including balconies, own building height, variable material reflectances, non-orthogonal room geometries, and regulatory validation. Two models are developed: a default-materials model with fixed reflectances for preliminary design checks, and a custom-materials model with variable project-specific reflectances. Both are trained on about 20,000 synthetic Radiance-simulated rooms and validated on a held-out synthetic dataset and 102 rooms from 18 real building models. The default-materials model achieves $δ_1$ = 83.4% and MAE = 0.11% DF on synthetic data, and on real buildings predicts BBR (Swedish) compliance area within 4.85 pp MAE (bias +2.31 pp) and TEK17 (Norwegian) mean room DF within 0.304% DF MAE (bias +0.287% DF). The custom-materials variant shows 1.7-2.4$\times$ higher error across all metrics. Both models concentrate accuracy in the BBR compliance-critical zone (DF < 1%), achieving a sub-1% DF MAE of 0.105% DF and Compliance IoU of 0.883 on real buildings. All cases where compliance is overestimated are borderline, within 10.7 pp of the BBR threshold (median 3.2 pp) and 0.36% DF of the TEK17 threshold (median 0.10% DF). The default-materials model is recommended as a replacement for preliminary daylight simulations; the custom-materials variant requires further development before use in final compliance verification.

Traffic-Context-Aware Driver Emotion Recognition with Expectation-Deviation-Driven Inference

Driver emotion provides an important indication of how drivers perceive and evaluate ongoing traffic situations, making reliable emotion recognition an important capability for effective human-vehicle interaction. Yet, most existing driver emotion recognition approaches rely primarily on in-cabin images and overlook how surrounding traffic interactions shape driver emotional responses. To address this limitation, we propose a traffic-context-aware driver emotion recognition (TADER) framework that integrates in-cabin signals with out-of-cabin traffic scenarios, modeling driver emotions as arising from expectation deviation represented by the discrepancy between predicted and observed vehicle trajectories. The proposed framework comprises two key components: (1) a vision-based base recognizer that extracts spatiotemporal representations from in-cabin facial images to infer driver emotions, and (2) a traffic-context-aware fusion module that quantifies expectation deviation from external traffic scenarios and integrates it into the base recognizer via attention-based embedding fusion. To overcome the scarcity of paired in-cabin and out-of-cabin data, we introduce a transfer learning strategy that transfers a trajectory prediction model trained on large-scale naturalistic datasets to smaller paired datasets. Extensive experiments demonstrate that TADER consistently outperforms state-of-the-art methods across both naturalistic and simulated driving environments by incorporating traffic context information into emotion recognition, resulting in improvements of 5% in accuracy on the AIDE and manD datasets.

E3tereo: Improving Stereo Matching at Depth Boundaries with Learned Geometric Edges

Depth boundaries remain a major source of error in stereo matching. For IGEV on Scene Flow, geometric edge pixels account for only 6% of evaluated pixels but about half of the total absolute disparity error. Photometric edges can respond to texture changes without corresponding depth discontinuities, making them unreliable cues for boundary refinement. We propose E3Stereo, which learns geometric edges from disparity supervision and combines these boundary cues with the backbone disparity and image details to predict full-resolution disparity corrections. The same edge map also emphasizes boundary errors during training, while joint optimization allows the edge predictor to adapt to disparity refinement. On the Scene Flow dataset, E3Stereo reduces end-point error (EPE) at depth boundaries by 9.5%-14.3% across RAFT-Stereo, IGEV, and MonSter, while also reducing overall EPE. After fine-tuning, our IGEV-based model improves upon IGEV on all eleven reported metrics across ETH3D, KITTI 2012, and KITTI 2015. The source code will be made publicly available upon acceptance.

Seeing the Options: Typed Decisions over Visual Candidates with Reusable Option Memory

Typed decision-making tasks in agents, such as UI element selection, product recommendation, and chart matching, require models to choose from a set of discrete candidates. While prior work demonstrates that text-only System One models achieve four key properties including candidate-order invariance, scalability, and cache reuse via separate request-candidate encoding, extending them to multimodal settings where candidates are visual remains underexplored. We introduce Iris, a framework that accommodates image, text, or hybrid candidates while preserving the core decision module. We prove that order invariance and exact cache reuse hold for any per-candidate encoder under Iris, and derive the closed-form latency reduction. We construct a visual-candidate benchmark from Multimodal-Mind2Web (K=16, deduplicated, order-shuffled, three generalization splits). Independent encoding (Iris) achieves 78.4 / 72.1 / 74.8 percent accuracy with zero-flip rate under candidate reordering. Joint encoding reaches 90.6 / 89.5 / 91.6 percent but suffers an 8 to 10 percent answer-flip rate when shuffled. To bridge this gap, we propose a design that encodes each candidate conditioned on the request state without inter-candidate cross-attention; it preserves strict order invariance, enables forked cache sharing, and attains 91.7 / 86.5 / 90.0 percent accuracy at one-third of OneJev latency with calibrated null probabilities (AUROC 0.93 to 0.97). Reusing image candidates across requests cuts latency by 37x at K=256 and 191x at K=2048. On MetaEncoder benchmarks, Iris-4B achieves 100 percent on JevBench original and easy, outperforming 30B MetaEncoder and 27B OpenJev. Finally, we resolve a systems bottleneck on Ampere GPUs: reformulating Qwen3.5 patch-embedding Conv3d into its exact matrix-product form accelerates processing by 350x. Code, data, and checkpoints: https://github.com/lllfx/Iris.

Authors: Fengxin Li

Scalable Unrolled Chains for MRI Reconstruction

Unrolled variational networks achieve state-of-the-art performance for accelerated MRI reconstruction, but standard end-to-end training becomes prohibitively memory intensive as the number and width of cascades increase. We propose a memory-efficient training framework that updates a randomly sampled cascade while retaining supervision from the final reconstruction loss. The error signal is propagated from the end of the chain to the sampled cascade using an activation-light backward pass that avoids storing full chain activations, thereby decoupling training memory from chain length. A time-conditioned network shared across cascades further keeps the parameter count independent of chain length. The backward pass is approximate only through frozen normalization statistics, and a trained exact gradient control shows the approximation leaves reconstruction quality unchanged. The framework remains trainable at chain lengths and cascade widths where standard end-to-end backpropagation exhausts GPU memory. On fastMRI single-coil knee and multi-coil brain reconstruction, our method consistently outperforms other local training approaches. On high-SNR knee slices, our method surpasses E2E-VarNet at $8\times$ and matches it at $4\times$, while on the complete brain validation set it surpasses E2E-VarNet at $8\times$ and achieves comparable performance at $4\times$, with fewer parameters, lower peak memory, and faster per-iteration training. Code is available at https://github.com/emehillaj/scalable-unrolled-mri.

PathKGen: Pathology Knowledge Assisted MLLM for Explainable Histopathology

Whole Slide Image (WSI) analysis serves as the fundamental method for the clinical diagnosis of cancer in digital histopathology. Recent advances in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have enabled state-of-the-art (SOTA) automated histopathology report generation. However, MLLMs reason purely through distributions encoded in their neural parameters, limiting the amount of knowledge and reasoning logic they can contain given the model scale. Their black-box reasoning poses another challenge to the human interpretation of intermediate reasoning. We address these issues by utilizing knowledge graphs to inject external structured knowledge for MLLMs to reason over, while making this reasoning component visible. Knowledge graphs are capable of partially externalizing the containment of medical knowledge, an area that remains underexplored in pathology report generation. We introduce PathKGen, a knowledge graph-assisted MLLM for histopathology report generation. PathKGen has two main branches: (1) a visual path that learns visual token inputs to the MLLM using pathology foundation models, and (2) a knowledge path that learns knowledge token inputs by extracting key morphological features of WSIs through querying a pathology-specific knowledge graph. A comprehensive evaluation on WSI-Bench dataset shows that PathKGen surpasses previous SOTA methods while revealing diagnostic reasoning as visually intuitive graphs. Due to its modular design, the proposed knowledge path is complementary to vision-aligned MLLMs and can be extended to other applications by curating domain-specific knowledge graphs to generate knowledge tokens.

Rescuing Your Pruning: Rethinking Visual Token Pruning and Post-Training Recovery in MLLMs

Visual token pruning substantially reduces the inference cost of multimodal large language models (MLLMs), yet existing methods are mostly training-free and plug-and-play. They treat the resulting performance drop as an inevitable cost of compression and pursue increasingly complex pruning rules. We rethink this view through two questions: how much of the loss can post-training rescue, and, building on this recovery, how can joint compression push pruning to its limit? We curate a 282K-sample post-training mixture covering general VQA, reasoning, OCR, and grounding, and post-train 36 configurations that drop or merge tokens under a unified protocol. Post-training rescues most of the loss, even for OCR and grounding, and its adaptation gain in closing the gap between the pruned and the dense model grows as the budget tightens. For joint compression, we find that the vision encoder, not the LLM, dominates prefill time in native-resolution MLLMs, so where tokens are removed matters more than how many: keeping 40% of tokens at the encoder runs faster than keeping 10% at the projector. Our best configuration prunes the encoder lightly and the projector aggressively, leaves the LLM intact, and keeps general understanding and reasoning at the dense level. It accelerates the LLM stage by 3.49x but the end-to-end time-to-first-token by only 1.54x, showing that LLM-stage speedups overstate end-to-end gains. We hope these findings offer insights for future research on joint visual token compression. Our data, protocol, and code will be released.

EXE: Egocentric Cross-Embodiment Pretraining via Factorized Latent Actions for VLA Models

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models are bottlenecked by the scarcity of robot data. Egocentric human manipulation videos offer an abundant, diverse, and semantically-rich alternative that inherently captures manipulation intent. However, the embodiment gap makes it difficult to extract action-relevant representations from these videos. From the perspective of action execution, human hands and robots share analogous manipulation primitives---reaching, grasping, placing---yet exhibit distinct kinematics tied to their embodiments. This might motivate our key approach: egocentric video pretraining can yield shared action representations capturing transferable manipulation skills, while human-specific motion patterns should be disentangled to avoid interfering with robot policy learning. To this end, we propose EXE, an Egocentric Cross(X)-Embodiment pretraining framework that learns a factorized latent action representation from egocentric human videos, separating shared manipulation priors from embodiment-specific signals. By leveraging the shared action space, the robot policy acquires generalizable manipulation semantics from diverse human demonstrations without being biased by human-specific kinematics. This shared knowledge transfers across embodiments with different kinematic structures, enabling the same pretraining to benefit both parallel grippers and dexterous hands. EXE yields consistent gains over baseline without pretraining on RoboTwin 2.0, RoboCasa GR1, and real-world manipulation tasks.

Tracking Is Not Sensing: Artifact-Aware Thermal Video Respiration Estimation in Head-Free Marmosets

Continuous physiological monitoring is essential for linking behavior, internal state, and neural activity in neuroscience and translational research. In common marmosets, a key nonhuman primate model for social neuroscience, respiratory rate provides a basic readout of arousal, stress, and physiological regulation. Contact sensors can provide accurate measurements, but they add preparation burden and may interfere with head-free behavioral protocols. Thermal video offers a noncontact alternative by capturing breathing-related temperature changes around the nose and mouth. However, in head-free marmosets, these cues are weak, localized, and easily corrupted by head motion, pose changes, and fur. Here we show that this setting exposes a broader challenge in video-based physiological sensing: visually reliable tracking does not necessarily imply physiologically valid measurement. Using synchronized thermal video and contact respiration recordings, we show that marmoset respiration is recoverable from thermal video but strongly ROI-sensitive. Under head-free motion, visually plausible nasal tracking can still produce traces inconsistent with contact respiration, demonstrating that tracking is not sensing. We therefore estimate respiratory rate from learned temporal signals extracted from thermal clips and introduce Selective Temporal Attenuation Regularization (STAR). STAR selectively attenuates high-motion thermal discontinuities through local temporal imputation during training, regularizing models toward respiration-consistent dynamics while leaving inference unchanged on raw video. Across multiple deep-learning backbones, STAR improves head-free estimation, reaching 2.23 bpm MAE compared with 20.76 bpm for tracked ROI analysis.

FreeView: Robust 3D Reconstruction from Uncurated Views

Reconstructing a coherent 3D scene from uncurated image collections remains challenging due to the presence of irrelevant or non-overlapping views. Recent work has shown that feed-forward reconstruction models contain internal view-consistency signals that support distractor rejection. However, reference-conditioned filtering relies on a curated clean reference, which is not guaranteed in practical image collections. To address this limitation, we introduce FreeView, a training-free framework that jointly selects a filtering reference and rejects distractor views from an uncurated collection. FreeView leverages collection-level consensus to identify a representative view that serves as a reliable reference for filtering. It then adapts view rejection to each input collection by combining complementary visual and geometric evidence. This approach preserves useful views under substantial appearance and viewpoint variations while rejecting distractors. FreeView integrates with both VGGT and pi^3, removing the need for a manually curated reference or manual calibration of view rejection. Extensive experiments across multiple datasets demonstrate high clean-view retention and distractor rejection rates, leading to reliable 3D reconstruction from uncurated views.

PreView: Enhancing GUI Grounding with Preprocessed Views and Cues

GUI grounding requires locating small controls in cluttered screenshots and identifying which control a natural-language instruction refers to. Cropping and enlargement can expose local detail, but the resulting image may still leave ambiguity about the intended target. We propose PreView, an auxiliary preprocessor that improves the inputs to an existing grounding model without further training that model. PreView prepares two complementary types of input features: task-relevant views for localization and candidate-specific cues for target understanding. A trained lightweight auxiliary model predicts and refines a center, crops the screenshot into a local view, and enlarges that view for zoomed-in grounding. It also prepares a visual cue (i.e., a red-circle marker with a location hint) and a textual cue describing the candidate's identity or function. The frozen grounding model uses the prepared inputs to predict the final click. Across five benchmarks, PreView improves the average grounding accuracy of Qwen3-VL-2B, OS-Atlas-4B, and Qwen3-VL-4B without further training these grounding models. Extensive analyses examine the contributions of views and cues, center refinement, and visual input budgets.

LENS: Likelihood Evaluation and Neighbor Smoothing for Attribute-Based Person Retrieval on Unseen Cameras

Attribute-based person retrieval ranks surveillance images from a binary query of semantic attributes, but mean Average Attribute Degree of Match (mADM) drops on unseen cameras as predicted probabilities shift. Common pipelines rank gallery images by their L1 distance to the query, which penalizes a confident single-attribute mismatch no more than small uncertainties across many attributes, while post-hoc recalibration fitted on source validation data fails to transfer because the per-attribute maps fitted on source data do not match those of new cameras. We propose LENS (Likelihood Evaluation and Neighbor Smoothing), combining fine-tuned DINOv3 and CLIP models with a distilled student. LENS regularizes mutually exclusive attribute groups with group cross-entropy in training, scores gallery images by the log-likelihood of the exact query, and smooths gallery logits over nearest neighbors using DINOv3 appearance features from the same forward pass. On held-out datasets, all three changes raise mADM: the likelihood score improves all six retrieval metrics for the main ensemble and mADM for all 48 development models, and training-free kNN smoothing improves all 46 tested development models. On the hidden test set of the UPAR 2027 benchmark, LENS reaches 68.17 mADM.

UniQuery: Unifying Vision Models with a Joint Query Head for Cross-Domain Pedestrian Attribute Recognition

Pedestrian Attribute Recognition (PAR) predicts semantic attributes of individuals, such as gender, age, clothing and accessories, and its descriptions support attribute-based person retrieval and person re-identification. However, accurately recognizing fine-grained attributes across domains remains challenging due to occlusion, appearance variations, and shifts in image distributions. Ensembles of independently trained foundation encoders combine their logits with fixed fusion weights, so every attribute uses the same mix and the encoders are never trained together. We propose UniQuery, which instead jointly fine-tunes DINOv3 and CLIP for attribute-specific fusion. We evaluate UniQuery on the benchmark of the UPAR Challenge 2027 at the WACV 2027 Real-World Surveillance workshop, where models are trained on PA100k, PETA and Market-1501 with a shared set of attributes. In leave-one-dataset-out experiments, UniQuery outperforms the independent ensemble in Challenge_Avg on both held-out datasets. Ablations show that learning fusion over independently trained encoders does not improve upon logit averaging in the mean over the two held-out datasets, while freezing the encoders lowers the Challenge_Avg of UniQuery. On the hidden test set of the benchmark, UniQuery achieves a Challenge_Avg of 0.8001.

DROA-CLIPSeg: Prompt-Guided Thin Crack Segmentation in Low-Light Conditions

Pavement crack segmentation under low light is degraded by reduced contrast, shadows, headlight glare, joints, stains, and wet seams. Existing crack segmentation methods mostly assume good illumination. Low-light enhancement methods improve visual quality, but they do not explicitly preserve crack topology. To address these limitations, we formulate low-light crack segmentation as a degradation-aware, prompt-grounded structural defect segmentation problem and propose DROA-CLIPSeg, a parameter-efficient framework that adapts a frozen CLIPSeg vision language backbone using two lightweight modules. QKV Low-Rank Adaptation is injected into the Q/K/V projections of the vision and text encoders to improve crack-specific image text alignment without backbone fine-tuning. Many real-world signals are corrupted by localized, structured perturbations whose distribution is unknown: an intermittent background noise in a music recording, a watermark stamped on a photograph, a cast shadow.
