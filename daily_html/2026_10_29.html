Cross-Primitive Consistency in Jev

Jev enables efficient decision-making with large language models (LLMs) through typed querying primitives, allowing the same semantic state to be expressed in different formats, such as binary and multiple-choice queries. However, the consistency of decisions across primitives has not been systematically evaluated. This work introduces PrimaCons, a four-axis benchmark for assessing cross-primitive consistency while preserving the semantics of the query. Evaluations across three Jev sizes reveal consistency violations across all four axes. Our analysis shows that these violations follow systematic patterns and that cross-primitive discrepancies predict decision failure with a ROC-AUC of 0.91. To mitigate these inconsistencies, we propose the Cross-Primitive Residual Calibrator (CPRC), which attaches a lightweight residual network to a frozen backbone and jointly optimizes predictive accuracy and cross-primitive consistency. Under a leave-one-domain-out protocol, CPRC reduces cross-primitive inconsistency by 58.57% and improves accuracy by +2.97%., demonstrating its effectiveness in both in-domain and out-of-domain settings. Our findings establish cross-primitive consistency as a key dimension for evaluating Jev and provide a practical approach to improving the reliability of its decisions. Code and benchmark is available at https://github.com/67L1/PrimaCons-CRPR.

TRIM: Temporal Run-Time Monitoring of 3D Object Detection via Activation Statistics

Reliable perception is essential to intelligent transportation systems, yet deep neural networks can silently miss safety-critical objects while continuing to produce apparently plausible outputs. Existing run-time monitors for 3D object detection generally assess each frame independently and therefore cannot exploit the temporal evolution of occlusion, point sparsity, and scene complexity. We present TRIM, a lightweight temporal monitor that detects missed-object failures without modifying or retraining the underlying detector. TRIM summarises intermediate activations using compact channel-wise statistics and processes short sequences of these summaries with a temporal convolutional network. Experiments on NuScenes show that temporal monitoring improves AUROC from 0.686 to 0.704 and reduces the false alarm rate at 95% recall from 0.818 to 0.759 compared with single-frame monitoring. Early and intermediate backbone features provide the strongest monitoring signal, while the temporal inference head requires only 0.159 ms on a CPU.

V-CAPE: Visual Consistency Assessment for Product Image Editing

Recent advances in generative image editing enable complex transformations such as background replacement, object manipulation, and pose adjustment from natural language instructions. However, verifying that these transformations preserve visual consistency, defined as the preservation of an object's intrinsic visual attributes, including geometry, texture, color, and fine-grained details, remains a largely unsolved problem. This challenge is particularly pronounced at scale, where manual inspection is infeasible and automated quality assessment (AutoQA) could become essential. In this work, we introduce Visual Consistency Assessment for Product Image Editing (V-CAPE), a large-scale dataset for evaluating visual consistency in generative image editing. The dataset consists of 40K unique source-target product image pairs, each annotated with binary accept/reject labels and structured failure modes capturing fine-grained inconsistencies such as geometric distortions, pose errors, and identity mismatches. V-CAPE combines real-world edited images with a synthetic subset constructed from 3D assets, enabling both realistic evaluation and controlled analysis. Vision-language models (VLMs) across all evaluated size categories on AutoQA achieve accuracy below 65%, indicating that the dataset poses a substantial challenge regardless of model scale. Our results highlight fundamental limitations of current models in verifying visual consistency under transformation, positioning V-CAPE as a benchmark for advancing fine-grained image editing evaluation.

Data Scaling Laws in Stereo Matching: From In-domain Fitting to Cross-domain Generalization

In the era of large models, stereo matching is developing towards generalizable foundation models trained on million-scale synthetic images. However, how model performance scales up with synthetic data is still underexplored. In this work, we preliminarily investigate the in-domain and cross-domain data scaling laws in stereo matching. A realistic synthetic dataset, named XStereo, containing 200 thousand samples is first built to serve as a high-quality benchmark for data scaling research. In-domain training on five large-scale datasets demonstrate a two-regime scaling behavior with data, transitioning from the standard power law to saturation, where the critical transition point is approximately invariant under a fixed model. Cross-domain performance exhibits a substantially weaker and less predictable dependence on the source-domain data scale. The distribution and coverage diversity are more effective for diminishing domain gaps. More instructive suggestions are provided for high-efficiency data construction and training. The insightful findings are significant verification and extension of the existing scaling laws in both language and vision, paving the way to efficient and stronger stereo matching. The XStereo dataset is public at https://huggingface.co/datasets/Tong-Zhao/XStereo.

SpectralTF-MAE: A Pre-trained Spectral Foundation Model for Tokamak Fusion Plasma Diagnostics

Spectroscopic diagnostics provide rich information for characterizing plasma states in tokamak fusion experiments, while existing learning-based approaches are often designed for individual tasks and rely heavily on labeled data. In this work, we propose SpectralTF-MAE, a self-supervised spectral foundation model for tokamak plasma diagnostics. The model is pretrained on paired charge-exchange recombination spectroscopy (CXRS) and X-ray crystal spectroscopy (XCS) measurements through masked spectral reconstruction. To better capture diagnostically important spectral structures, we introduce a peak-aware masking strategy together with a peak-enhanced reconstruction objective, encouraging the model to focus on characteristic spectral regions. A shared Transformer encoder is employed to learn transferable representations across different diagnostic modalities. The pretrained model is further adapted to several downstream tasks, including plasma-parameter prediction, cross-diagnostic spectral generation, and spectral peak analysis. Experiments on EAST diagnostic data demonstrate that the proposed framework achieves competitive performance across different tasks and consistently benefits from self-supervised spectral pretraining. These results indicate the potential of SpectralTF-MAE as a general and transferable representation learning framework for plasma spectroscopic diagnostics.

RoSAMDepth: Robust Self-supervised Depth Estimation Leveraging Segment Anything Model

Robust depth estimation aims to maintain high-quality depths across diverse conditions. However, most existing methods estimate depth without taking into account the object-level information. As a result, the predicted depth may easily deviate within objects and become blurred under adverse conditions. To overcome this weakness, we propose RoSAMDepth, a novel framework that can assist robust self-supervised depth estimation in leveraging rich and diverse object-level priors from the Segment Anything Model (SAM). We focus on incorporating object-level information across three key aspects: a segment-guided representation contrasting method that injects object-level awareness into the feature representation space; an adaptive regional outlier masking strategy combined with a regional Gaussian likelihood loss that enforces regional depth smoothness; and an object-level reliability estimation strategy that mitigates the influence of unreliable supervision. Extensive experiments across multiple datasets and diverse weather conditions demonstrate that our method produces sharper, more accurate depth predictions, consistently outperforming state-of-the-art methods. Our code is available at https://github.com/xagao/RoSAMDepth.

Detecting Fine-Grained Differences between Image Datasets

Detecting whether and in particular how two data distributions differ is a fundamental statistical problem. For example, we might be interested in which concepts (expressed in natural language) differ between two classes, the outputs of different generative models, or the same model with different prompt specifications. When working with large and complex image datasets, we intuitively think that first selecting concepts from a broad list using fine-grained patch embeddings, and then optimizing the concepts to better fit the data is an effective workflow. However, prior work instead uses MLLMs to propose concepts and image-level embeddings to select them. Therefore, we systematically compare these techniques and find that selecting concepts from broad lists can replace MLLM-proposals at obviously lower cost. MLLM inference can then be spent on optimizing selected concepts instead to achieve substantial improvements. Additionally, by designing a new benchmark that focuses on low-prevalence and multi-concept distinctions, we find that selecting nonredundant concepts instead of only the most distinguishing concepts matters, and we introduce techniques for how to effectively do this. Finally, we show how our methods address important problems like detecting spurious correlations and biases. Overall, our work presents a fundamental perspective shift on how we analyze interpretable distributional differences between datasets.

Authors: Leander Girrbach, Alexander von Recum, Kirill Bykov, Zeynep Akata

From the Superderivative to a Relative-Gradient Edge Operator

First-derivative edge detectors such as Sobel, Prewitt, Roberts and Scharr measure absolute grey-level differences. We revisit the log-gradient magnitude, a relative-gradient edge operator, and motivate it by analogy with the superderivative, a multiplicative derivative in which the independent variable is perturbed multiplicatively rather than additively. In one and two dimensions the logarithm of the superderivative is a radial logarithmic derivative; its local, origin-free, isotropic analogue is the magnitude of the logarithmic gradient. The resulting operator measures relative rather than absolute change, giving an edge-strength map that is invariant under global intensity rescaling. We present the theory, show how the operator can be implemented in two steps---a classical gradient kernel plus a pointwise division---and compare it with standard first-derivative filters on synthetic images with ground-truth edges and on the official BSDS500 benchmark. We also study a parametric family that interpolates between the absolute gradient and the log-gradient; empirically, the best members are small intermediate exponents, which edge the classical kernels by a small but statistically detectable margin in the per-image metric and, with the same Gaussian smoothing as the single-$σ$ Canny, match it in ODS and lead it in OIS and AP, while the tuned Canny remains ahead in ODS. The single-threshold margin over the kernels does not replicate on the held-out test split, and the fully relative endpoint is never the best member, its ranking depending on the edge-map normalisation. The contribution is the construction and the invariance it buys, not a new state of the art.

Self-Consistency Geometry Injection for Multi-view Consistent Image Generation

Recent diffusion models have achieved impressive progress in single image to multi-view generation by synthesizing consistent novel views efficiently. Existing methods usually explicitly or implicitly couple geometry inference and texture synthesis, making generation vulnerable to inaccurate geometry prediction. Although several geometry-guided methods have explored decoupling, they usually treat predicted geometry as a fixed condition and do not explicitly model whether the geometry is reliable. We therefore propose Reliability-Guided Multi-View Generation (RGMV), which comprises a parameter-free Self-Consistency Geometry Gate (SCG) and a Reliability-aware Geometry Injection (RGI) module. Specifically, a geometry proposal generator produces multiple geometry predictions for the same reference image and target viewpoints under different stochastic sampling configurations. SCG measures their spatial agreement to derive a reliability map, which RGI uses to modulate the injection strength of geometry-conditioned residuals in the RGB synthesis model. Moreover, RGMV can adapt to different geometry proposal generators. As a result, RGMV preserves stronger geometric guidance in stable regions while attenuating unreliable geometry cues before they propagate into multi-view RGB generation. Extensive experiments show that reliability-aware geometry injection improves the geometric quality of multi-view generation. Code: https://github.com/WISLab-GDUT/RGMV.

The Power Watershed as a Differentiable Segmentation Layer

Can a Watershed transform, the classical tool of morphological segmentation, be placed at the end of a deep network and trained through? The Power Watershed (PW) is the limit of the seeded Random Walker (RW) as p grows to infinity with edge weights raised to the power p. At every finite p the RW solves a sparse positive-definite linear system and is exactly differentiable with respect to the edge weights and the unaries through one adjoint solve; its multi-label form costs one right-hand side per label, and its limit is the PW. We study this family as the last layer of a segmentation network, the watershed layer (WS layer). A conditioning study measures how the solve, the gradient and the distance to the limit evolve with p, and the implicit gradient is checked against unrolling. On synthetic data with correct edge costs the WS layer transforms a soft prediction into an exact boundary. On nuclei segmentation (PanNuke), a binary WS layer is level with a thresholded U-Net. With the instances as labels, the WS layer turns the U-Net into an instance segmenter whose number of labels is set per image by the markers rather than by the output layout, and the network is trained through that partition: instance F1 score rises from 0.656 to 0.795 over a U-Net with connected components, level with the same network followed by a tuned marker watershed. At the training temperature the partition is a watershed of the learned costs, and the finite temperature lets the solve hedge, discounting weak markers and sharing contested pixels. The hedging puts the WS layer 0.015--0.020 F1 ahead of the baseline when trained on a tenth of the data, at the price of having to deploy the finite-p solve rather than its exact limit at inference. Code and the full experimental record are released.

Similar Scores, Opposite Failures: Human Correction as an Instrument for Video Caption Evaluation

Video captioning models are increasingly evaluated with metrics designed for single-sentence image captions. We test whether those metrics recover human judgement on dense, multi-window video description, and find that they do not. Six frontier models captioned 196 short clips. Trained annotators post-edited every caption against a failure taxonomy fixed before annotation, once as a whole-clip overview and once window by window, and a quality-control reviewer checked each annotation, yielding 1,144 overview verdicts and 4,672 window verdicts. Reference-overlap metrics do not separate the captions reviewers accepted from those they rejected: BLEU-4 reaches an AUC of 0.510 and a CIDEr-style tf-idf cosine 0.528, and restricting references to reviewer corrections leaves BLEU-4 at 0.479 (95% CI 0.431-0.529), indistinguishable from chance. Review granularity changes what is detected. The same annotators record camera and shot errors on 2.7% of captions when reading the overview and on 12.8% when reading window by window, a 4.7x gap that holds across every robustness check we ran, on the mode most likely to need a near-total rewrite. Correct-overview rates span 54.1-72.7%, but only 4 of 15 pairwise gaps survive Bonferroni correction (5 after adjusting for reviewer and clip effects), and accuracy and derived severity order the models differently. Models with statistically indistinguishable accuracy have sharply different failure profiles, which no single scalar shows. We argue that caption evaluation should record what was wrong rather than only how often, and we report the taxonomy, the per-model profiles, the corpus properties that predict difficulty, and the code that regenerates every number in the paper.

UnMixMamba: Unmixing Guided State Space Subpixel Mapping of Crops from MODIS Time Series

Subpixel mapping (SPM) of crop types from \SI{250}{m} Moderate Resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer (MODIS) time series to field-scale Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada (AAFC) labels supports acreage estimation and agricultural monitoring. Three difficulties remain: (i)~spectral-temporal coupling and mixed pixels that break direct coarse-to-fine classifiers; (ii)~intra-class endmember variability that a single spectrum per crop cannot capture; and (iii)~uniform refinement on the high-resolution (HR) grid, which treats every subpixel the same, so most of the effort goes to easy pixels while errors remain in the minority class. UnMixMamba addresses them as follows. For~(i), a temporal-spectral-spatial decoupled (TSSD) factorization estimates simplex fractions and drives AAFC classification from the same mixture field, instead of mapping low-resolution (LR) observations directly to HR logits. For~(ii), \(\times 8\) abundance super-resolution and a learnable per-crop variant endmember bank reconstruct the MODIS cube at LR and HR under a linear mixing model. For~(iii), the predicted crop fractions decide where a fixed Mamba token budget is spent, so HR refinement focuses on the minority class instead of the full grid. On Alberta 2015 under a fixed spatial-block protocol, UnMixMamba reaches OA 0.8397, mIoU 0.6640, AA 0.7837, and Kappa 0.7715, ahead of ST-DRes, ESPCN, FineCrop, AgriFM, and classical SPM baselines. The task is closed-set four-crop mapping inside a known agricultural mask.

Authors: Motasem Alkayid, Yimin Zhu, Naser El-Sheimy, Lincoln Linlin Xu

Residual Motion Splatting for Continuous Space-Time Video Super-Resolution

Continuous space-time video super-resolution produces a high-resolution, highframe-rate video from a low-resolution, low-frame-rate one at arbitrary spatial scales and temporal instants. Classical spatial multi-frame super-resolution has shown that detail beyond a single frame's sampling limit can be recovered by aligning complementary observations with sub-pixel precision and accumulating them on a finer grid. Existing learning-based methods largely depart from this principle: some align and fuse frames only at low resolution before regressing a continuous high-resolution representation, while others interpolate each frame to the output resolution before splatting. In both cases, the per-frame samples are resampled before fusion, so their sub-pixel positions are lost before frames are combined. Restoring this principle in a learned continuous model raises two challenges: estimating sub-pixel motion at unobserved times, and handling holes and occlusions when splatting a coarse grid onto a finer one. We address the first by predicting per-pixel continuous trajectories that refine linear optical-flow paths with smooth residual motion, using features integrated over the full video sequence. We address the second by directly splatting consecutive low-resolution features in a learned latent space onto a high-resolution grid at their predicted sub-pixel positions with predicted visibility weights, before a decoder resolves remaining ambiguities. This makes temporal interpolation and multi-frame superresolution a single operation. Our method improves both reconstruction fidelity and perceptual quality on multiple standard benchmarks over the state of the art, while running up to an order of magnitude faster.

Vision-Based Density Estimation for Adaptive Traffic Control

Traffic congestion is becoming a critical challenge in rapidly growing urban centers, particularly in developing nations where rapid urbanization and inadequate transport infrastructure place increasing strain on transport networks. In highly heterogeneous and chaotic traffic flows, camera-based traffic-state estimation is limited by severe vehicle occlusion and overlap. In this paper, we propose a framework to optimize traffic flow directly from stochastic visual observations. The architecture is structurally decoupled into three primary components: (a) vehicle density estimation, (b) predictive dynamics modeling, and (c) policy optimization. The proposed methodology introduces a convolutional neural network (CNN) that learns the mapping from camera feeds to spatial density maps, utilizing an adaptive k-nearest-neighbor (k-NN) Gaussian kernel to handle varying congestion levels. This feeds into an ST3DNet predictive module for spatiotemporal forecasting, followed by a hierarchical Graph-Transformer Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) algorithm to optimize signal phases. The density estimation stage was evaluated on 40 annotated intersection images from Rambagh 4, Jaipur, India, with a reported five-fold validation mean absolute error (MAE) of $3.32 \pm 0.95$ vehicles and a mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) of 14.5%. These results pertain to the perception component; empirical training and evaluation of the forecasting and control stages remain future work.

Authors: Mohammad Usaid, Deepak Silaych, Hrithik Mhatre, Archak Mittal

Calibrating Track-Query Updates at Test Time for Multi-Object Tracking

Multi-Object Tracking (MOT) requires localizing multiple objects and maintaining their identities over time. Recent query-propagation trackers carry target-specific representations across frames and update them with current-frame image features, yet identity association remains difficult when visually similar targets overlap or cross. During such interactions, current-frame query updates may contain components directed toward nearby active tracks, which may affect identity association in later frames. To investigate this issue, we analyze track-query updates preceding identity switches (IDSWs), where the same target is assigned a different track ID. Our analysis shows that (i) propagated track-query representations change more abruptly before IDSWs, (ii) current-frame updates have larger positive projections along directions toward other active tracks, and (iii) bounding-box intersection over union (IoU) retrieves the counterpart track involved in a subsequent IDSW with high recall when that track remains active before the switch. Motivated by these findings, we propose Test-time Track-Query Calibration (TQC), a training-free and gradient-free method for query-propagation trackers. TQC calibrates the tracker output before propagation using a bounded correction estimated from update components directed toward nearby active tracks. It requires only track-query representations and bounding boxes available during inference, without retraining or architectural modification. Across nine DanceTrack and SportsMOT test settings, TQC improves HOTA and AssA in all settings and IDF1 in eight, with an average HOTA gain of +0.38 and limited inference overhead. Controlled variants on six validation settings further show that TQC's correction direction yields more consistent HOTA improvements than alternative directions with matched correction norms.

Geometry-Aware Trajectory State Modeling for Few-Shot Action Recognition

Few-shot action recognition (FSAR) requires representations that expose discriminative motion dynamics from only a few labeled videos. Recent trajectory-based methods leverage long-term point tracks for explicit temporal correspondence, but often treat trajectories as carriers of sampled visual features or summarize their motion with compact 2D displacement-based descriptors. We instead view an action as the ordered evolution of 2.5D trajectory states and their local geometric interactions. Based on this view, we introduce GeoTRAM, a geometry-aware trajectory state modeling framework for FSAR. GeoTRAM lifts sparse tracks into 2.5D trajectories, where StateMotion models ordered intra-trajectory state evolution and RelMotion captures state-conditioned local interactions through time-varying relative geometry and motion. Quality-filtered spectral sampling provides more reliable trajectory initialization; complementary semantic, motion, and depth cues are separately refined and preserved for joint spatio-temporal modeling; action-guided reweighting and top-$ρ$ trajectory selection support complementary frame- and trajectory-level matching. Experiments on five FSAR benchmarks demonstrate strong gains on the motion-centric Something-Something benchmarks while maintaining competitive performance on more appearance-driven benchmarks and a favorable accuracy--efficiency trade-off.

Automatic Iconographic Annotation of Medieval Illuminations: Comparing Generative Vision-Language Models and Supervised Discriminative Classifiers

The large-scale digitisation of medieval manuscripts creates a growing need for automated iconographic indexing. We evaluate two complementary paradigms for this task on 9,735 full-folio images annotated with 2,333 TIMEL concepts: generative Vision-Language Models (VLM) and supervised discriminative classifiers (SDC). The VLM experiments evaluate fine-tuning, structured JSON decoding, taxonomybased candidates generation, and deterministic reranking. The discriminative experiments evaluate flat, hierarchy-aware, and patch-attention heads over a frozen DINOv3 encoder. Our results highlight key functional differences: ML-Decoder (SDC) achieves the strongest overall retrieval performance (precision@1 of 0.696, MRR of 0.806), with loss calibration (Asymmetric Loss) serving as the primary performance lever. While SDCs provide superior precision and reliability within a closed vocabulary, VLMs offer strong semantic generalization and broad taxonomic coverage, particularly through structured JSON targets and verify-calibrate reranking (hierarchical F1 of 0.559). Furthermore, model behavior strongly depends on concept abstraction: SDCs excel at perceptually grounded descriptive concepts, whereas VLMs provide useful hypotheses for complex interpretative scenes. Finally, we find that enforcing explicit hierarchy within model architectures limits coverage compared to spatial feature aggregation. Overall, rather than competing approaches, SDCs and VLMs emerge as complementary components for future hybrid indexing systems combining structured retrieval with exploratory annotation assistance. As a methodological contribution, we define the soft F1 metric to provide a taxonomy-aware evaluation that credits semantically close predictions based on lowest common ancestors.

IntReal: A Real-World Dataset and Benchmark for Multimodal Interleaved Generation

Humans routinely consume multimodal content, where textual and visual information is naturally interleaved. Although Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) can jointly generate text and images, multimodal interleaved generation remains largely underexplored. Progress in this area is hindered by two key limitations: (1) the lack of well-defined interleaved generation tasks and corresponding training data, and (2) the absence of fine-grained evaluation protocols that can reliably diagnose model performance across different capability dimensions. To this end, we present IntReal, comprising two complementary components: IntReal-100K, a real-world dataset of multimodal interleaved samples spanning four major categories, and the IntReal Evaluation Suite, which evaluates model performance across seven complementary dimensions using specialized expert models, enabling coarse-to-fine diagnosis. Extensive experiments validate our evaluation suite through low inter-metric redundancy (max pairwise absolute Spearman's $ρ\leq 0.42$ ), high agreement with human judgments (83.3% accuracy), and strong consistency across different expert evaluators (Spearman's $ρ> 0.9$ ), supporting the complementarity and reliability of our metrics. By benchmarking four families of UMMs, we show that IntReal Evaluation Suite reveals distinct strengths and weaknesses across models. Post-training experiments demonstrate the utility of IntReal-100K for improving interleaved generation and further show that training unimodal generation can also benefit interleaved generation. These findings suggest that interleaved generation can benefit from modality-specific optimization, rather than requiring joint text-image training at every stage.

Structure-retained low-rank adapters for weather synthesis

Real-world deployment of computer vision models, particularly in outdoor scenarios, is often limited by environmental interferences, such as adverse weather. Improving model robustness across weather conditions is thus critical for reliability. However, sourcing high-quality data relevant to isolated weather effects is a significant challenge. Most datasets include changes in dynamic elements across observations of the same scene: subjects such as pedestrians and vehicles change between shots, while camera viewpoints vary. In this work, we address this with a novel weather synthesis framework that utilizes structure-retaining Low-Rank Adapters (LoRA). Experimental results demonstrate that our method improves upon existing synthesis approaches, achieving greater realism while preserving structure. This provides a robust pipeline for generating high-fidelity training and evaluation data for outdoor computer vision applications.

Visually Irrelevant Hallucinations in Medical Vision-Language Models

Medical vision-language models (VLMs) show strong performance in radiology report generation (RRG) and medical visual question answering (Med-VQA), producing free-form clinical descriptions and answers from medical images. However, they may generate clinically meaningful statements that lack visual evidence or contradict image content. We refer to these errors as visually irrelevant hallucinations and investigate them at the phrase level. We propose CLEAR (Counterfactual Localized Evaluation of Alignment and Response), a model-agnostic post-hoc framework for phrase-level hallucination risk estimation. CLEAR decomposes generated responses into visually assessable clinical phrases. For each phrase, phrase-conditioned grounding identifies a candidate visual region, which is selectively perturbed by a localized counterfactual intervention. CLEAR characterizes the intervention using two complementary auditing cues: response cues, capturing semantic and clinically meaningful changes in the generated response, and alignment cues, measuring changes in image-phrase compatibility. These cues are normalized and jointly modeled to estimate phrase-level hallucination risk. Across four datasets spanning RRG and Med-VQA, CLEAR outperforms state-of-the-art hallucination detection baselines across multiple medical and general-purpose VLMs. On MIMIC-CXR, CLEAR improves AUPRC from 85.10% to 90.23%. Its effectiveness is further validated on a GT-derived controlled VIH benchmark. These results establish visually irrelevant hallucination as an important fine-grained reliability failure mode in medical VLMs and demonstrate the value of model-agnostic visual-support auditing for more trustworthy multimodal clinical generation.

SparseTokenGS: Gaussian-based Sparse-View CBCT Reconstruction via Reusable Token Pool

Sparse-view cone-beam computed tomography is widely used for fast, low-dose volumetric imaging and lesion localization in dentistry and image-guided interventions, but it introduces severe artifacts. Feed-forward Gaussian methods transfer the reconstruction cost to training and decode each coordinate from projection-aligned features and neighboring anchors. However, information from the whole scan is written only when the field is constructed and cannot be retrieved during coordinate decoding, leaving the volume inconsistent within the scan. This is a common limitation of such methods: once the field is fixed, decoding uses only local features and reconstruction quality drops substantially. We propose SparseTokenGS, a Gaussian-based method for sparse-view cone-beam computed tomography reconstruction via a reusable token pool. Anchor Token Fusion and Point-Token Attention query this shared pool at the anchors and at the query coordinates, respectively. The local Gaussian prior is retained, and the volume is reconstructed in one forward pass, without per-scan optimization or a projection-domain loss. On three public chest and dental datasets, SparseTokenGS achieves the highest reconstruction performance among the compared methods. On clinical dental scans, reconstruction from only six projections reaches 29.51 decibels in peak signal-to-noise ratio and a structural similarity of 0.842 against the full-view reference, and off-the-shelf tooth-and-pulp segmentation keeps these structures separable for root-canal localization. Experiments on volcanic rock and chicken show performance after same-object adaptation. A reusable token pool gives feed-forward reconstruction a mechanism that keeps scan-wide context available through decoding and can benefit future rapid clinical lesion localization and analysis. The code is available at: https://github.com/FrankDengAI/SparseTokenGS.

Volley3D: Unified 3D Full-Sequence Reconstruction from Monocular Broadcasts

Unlike widely studied racket sports, team sports such as volleyball feature unpredictable multi-contact sequences and high altitude jumps. These dynamics categorically invalidate the standard planar projection assumptions relied upon by existing methods. To resolve these limitations without 3D training supervision, we propose a complete, physics-grounded pipeline tailored to monocular broadcast volleyball. First, we include a correction module to the perspective drift induced by ground-plane projection by coupling temporal jump spotting with Newtonian interpolation for 3D player placement. Second, for 3D ball trajectory estimation, we design a dedicated sub-trajectory segmentation architecture alongside a collision-matching algorithm. By anchoring physical equations to reconstructed 3D hand vertices, we lift noisy ball 2D detections into a spatiotemporally coherent 3D scene. Evaluations on multi-camera benchmarks demonstrate our approach significantly reduces 3D spatial drift compared to 2D-reprojection baselines.

Sparse tensor networks for individual tree diameter estimation in mixed forests

Measurements of tree diameter at breast height (DBH) are fundamental to forest inventories, but manual surveys across entire stands or regions are impractical. UAV-borne laser scanning (ULS) enables scalable automated forest surveys, yet canopy occlusion makes accurate DBH estimation from ULS point clouds non-trivial. Limited stem-level point density of ULS data constrains approaches relying on detailed stem reconstruction, such as Quantitative Structure Models (QSMs) and circle fitting. Empirical machine learning methods based on predefined point cloud metrics are an alternative. New deep learning approaches offer an even more principled solution, allowing models to learn directly from point clouds. Sparse tensor networks have shown particular promise, transforming forestry tasks such as tree species classification. We investigate whether these strengths can be extended to estimate individual tree DBH from ULS data across four mixed-species temperate forest plots in Germany. We further advance existing sparse tensor networks by introducing TreeNext, a lightweight deep learning architecture for direct individual tree parameter estimation from voxelized LiDAR point clouds. We present a fully automated forest inventory pipeline from individual tree instance segmentation to DBH regression, and quantify the impact of segmentation errors by comparing performance on automatically segmented and ground truth instances. TreeNext achieves an $R^2$ of 0.8355 on automatically segmented trees and 0.9380 on ground truth segments, outperforming strong tabular baselines including Random Forest and XGBoost. Segmentation quality emerges as the dominant error source in the automated pipeline. TreeNext proves highly data-efficient, performing well even in small data scenarios. Our findings establish sparse tensor networks as a practical foundation for automated individual tree DBH estimation from ULS.
