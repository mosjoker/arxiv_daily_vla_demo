Scene-Adaptive Readout and Decoding in Diffusion Driving VLAs

Diffusion driving vision-language-action models repeatedly execute the backbone at every denoising stage, incurring substantial inference cost, while it remains unclear whether all scenes require the same decoding computation. To distinguish when a trajectory becomes readable from how much further refinement it requires, we analyze trajectory readout across network depth and decoding progress. We identify a depth-decoding dissociation in which late-layer representations already support useful trajectory readout after the first forward sweep, whereas early-layer representations benefit more from continued decoding. We further find that both the preferred readout layer and decoding depth vary across scenes. Based on these observations, we propose a scene-adaptive readout and decoding framework. A scene-conditioned readout MoE adaptively weights and combines trajectory predictions from layer-specific late-layer representations after a single backbone sweep, while an adaptive decoding policy applies additional native refinement only when required by the scene. We evaluate our framework on two diffusion driving VLAs across the Waymo E2E and nuScenes benchmarks. Our method improves validation RFS by up to 0.56 while also improving test performance, and achieves an end-to-end speedup of up to $6.0\times$ on Waymo E2E, while consistently reducing trajectory error on nuScenes. These results demonstrate that scene-level readout and decoding allocation improves planning quality while avoiding unnecessary decoding. Our code is available at https://github.com/sjyu001/SARD

Do Embodied VLMs Actually Use Their Scene-Graph Memory?

Embodied agents that answer questions about their route need a memory of what they saw, and scene graphs are a natural form for it. A vision-language model (VLM) can receive such a graph as text or through a graph encoder, but accuracy alone cannot show whether the graph holds the episode's facts, reaches the model, or is actually relied on. In this diagnostic study, we introduce a graph-swap test for the last of these: each episode is given another episode's graph, and we count how many answers change. On an embodied question-answering benchmark with routes through two to seven rooms, we use it to compare ways of delivering scene-graph memory to a fine-tuned VLM. As text, the memory is used strongly, adding 30 points on route questions. As a graph, it is not, and we trace why: the encoder preserves the information, but standard pooled and late fusion discard it. Giving each node its own input token recovers part of it, a gain of 8.3 points on route questions that the swap test confirms comes from the graph. The test also exposes a shortcut that accuracy hides: when the same facts appear in the text, the model ignores its graph. The test thus offers a cheap check of whether a VLM uses its scene-graph memory, and our analysis points to how that memory should be delivered.

Learning to Look: A Foundation for Visual Understanding

Vision-language models (VLMs) suffer from visual hallucination, such as saying an object that is not present in an image. We ask a more fundamental question: whether a VLM is even aware of where it is looking before understanding the semantic meaning in the image. We formulate this question as a LOOK task: identifying which parts of its visual input were available to the model. We construct the task in a self-supervised way without manually labeling the available region to the model. Our experiments show that existing VLMs fail to localize the visible region. We further show that fine-tuning VLMs on the proposed LOOK task improves a model's awareness of visual input.

UniGS-SLAM: Monocular Gaussian SLAM with Enhanced Geometry and Open-Vocabulary Semantics

Monocular semantic SLAM aims to reconstruct scene geometry and build semantic maps from RGB observations. Existing Gaussian-based methods often attach semantic labels or features to evolving Gaussian primitives, making semantic fusion difficult to maintain and increasing reconstruction overhead. Hybrid Gaussian--voxel representations provide more stable semantic support, but often rely on RGB-D input or known poses and do not revise historical semantic evidence after geometry updates. We present UniGS-SLAM, an RGB-only framework that decouples open-vocabulary semantics from Gaussian reconstruction while preserving bidirectional interaction. Foundation priors enhance monocular depth and pose estimation, while semantic observations are fused into an independent sparse voxel map with observation provenance, enabling historical contributions to be revised as reconstruction evolves. The fused semantics further guide Gaussian filtering and local surface refinement. Experiments on Replica, ScanNet, and KITTI-360 show leading tracking, rendering, and geometric reconstruction performance among representative RGB-only methods, together with strong open-set semantic mapping on Replica and ScanNet. We also achieve a 2.37-fold reconstruction speedup over the fastest compared semantic SLAM baseline. Project page: https://3dagentworld.github.io/UniGS-SLAM/.

Unified Loss-Aware Density Control for 3D Gaussian Splatting

The efficiency and fidelity of 3D Gaussian Splatting critically depend on adaptive density control, which grows and suppresses Gaussian primitives throughout optimization. However, the standard pipeline still relies on heuristic view-space gradients, scale thresholds, and global opacity resets, rather than explicitly assessing the loss-reducing effect of each split, clone, or opacity update. This mismatch can allocate primitives to already-sufficient regions, miss under-reconstructed structures, and destabilize training through blanket opacity changes. In this work, we recast adaptive density control as a collection of local loss-reduction decisions. We derive efficient Taylor-based per-Gaussian surrogates that predict the objective change caused by three primitive-level operations: splitting, cloning, and opacity decay. This yields a unified loss-aware framework with three components. First, we extend second-order splitting to a normalized joint position-scale space, enabling children to adapt not only their locations but also their anisotropic extents. Second, we formulate duplicate cloning as a surrogate minimization problem, selecting only Gaussians whose duplicated contribution is predicted to improve reconstruction and assigning each child a principled opacity. Third, we replace global opacity reset with selective soft opacity decay that weakens harmful primitives while preserving useful ones. Across standard 3DGS benchmarks, our method produces more targeted primitive allocation and achieves improved rendering quality with comparable or smaller Gaussian budgets compared to baselines.

Scalar Smoothness Is One Point on a Besov Spectrum

Studies of neural network generalization and robustness often summarize smoothness with a single number, such as a Lipschitz constant or a Hessian curvature statistic. However, Lipschitz constants summarize global worst-case sensitivity and Hessian statistics summarize local curvature; neither captures how model responses vary with the size, direction, and location of perturbations encountered in real-world deployment. To measure smoothness under these perturbations, we introduce a finite-difference roughness spectrum that profiles model responses across these conditions. Our roughness spectrum generalizes scalar smoothness measures, bringing Lipschitz sensitivity and directional Hessian curvature into a common framework across perturbation scales. We connect this spectrum to classical Besov theory, which describes how a function's variation changes with scale. Our theoretical analysis also proves that a larger output shift can cause less task damage than a smaller one, and that local curvature alone can miss the blocks most vulnerable to finite perturbations. In image classification, Roughness Source Control (RSC) suppresses margin roughness along noise directions and improves noise robustness in a comparison of 80 checkpoints across four model--dataset combinations. In post-training quantization, we identify blocks that need higher precision by measuring task-loss increases from perturbations matched to the size and direction of each block's quantization error. Our mixed-precision allocation achieves lower perplexity than the Hessian-based HAWQ-V2 baseline under the same GPTQ weights and bit budgets in all 14 settings across seven held-out large language models.

4D Triangle Splatting Reconstruction of Dynamic Endoscopic Scenes from Monocular Videos

Robust 4D reconstruction of deformable endoscopic scenes from monocular videos remains challenging due to non-rigid tissue deformation, illumination changes, occlusions, and unknown camera trajectories. Reliable dynamic reconstruction of endoscopies could significantly enhance visualization, diagnostic accuracy and intraoperative guidance of minimally invasive procedures. We propose SurgicalTS, a self-supervised triangle-splatting framework for dynamic endoscopic scene reconstruction from static monocular cameras. The scene is represented by a canonical set of triangles jointly optimized with a deformation network. We introduce a quaternion-based deformation model, an improved color parametrization, spatio-temporal priors, and a redefined initialization strategy that improves completeness under occlusions. Extensive evaluation on challenging in-vivo endoscopy videos from diverse procedures shows that SurgicalTS successfully models soft-tissue deformations and tool-tissue interactions over time, while consistently outperforming state-of-the-art methods. Our method also reduces training times while maintaining competitive geometric and photometric accuracy results, highlighting the effectiveness of quaternion-based deformation modelling with physical consistency for robust and efficient 4D reconstruction of endoscopic scenes.

Reconstruction as Spatial Evaluation: Diagnosing Cross-View Spatial Intelligence in Large Vision-Language Models

The spatial intelligence of large vision-language models (LVLMs) requires the ability to combine local visual observations into a geometrically consistent representation of the physical world. Many static benchmarks assess isolated spatial judgments, such as relative direction or distance, while interactive embodied evaluations combine perception with exploration, planning, control, and simulator-specific execution. To bridge this gap, we introduce RECONSPATIAL, a benchmark centered on multi-view 3D scene reconstruction across indoor, tabletop, and outdoor environments. Given real multi-view images and target-object queries, LVLMs are required to jointly predict oriented 3D bounding boxes in a shared, camera-anchored metric coordinate frame. Reconstructions are evaluated along two complementary axes: Structure for relative layout and Metric for physical scale and camera-anchored placement. Nine atomic spatial QA tasks, grouped into Geometric Perception and Spatial Relations, probe spatial capabilities involved in joint reconstruction. Offline navigation extends evaluation to path planning. Evaluation of 18 frontier LVLMs reveals a consistent gap between relative scene structure and metric reconstruction; even GPT-6-Astra scores 81.4% on Structure but only 58.7% on Metric. Tracing this gap, diagnostic analyses link atomic spatial capabilities to reconstruction quality and show that relational consistency alone does not ensure metric accuracy. Experiments with 3D map injection further separate scene construction from path planning, revealing how these geometric errors limit navigation success. Finally, spatial fine-tuning yields broad transfer across both spatial and general vision-language benchmarks.

NAS-PO: Native Attention-Strategy Policy Optimization for Vision-Language Models

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) has become an effective post-training paradigm for vision-language models (VLMs). Standard RLVR, however, assigns identical outcome-derived credit to all generated tokens regardless of their perceptual relevance. Recent perception-aware methods estimate visual reliance through image perturbation or masking. These indirect proxies require perturbation branches, while corruption sensitivity does not necessarily identify the visual evidence used for reasoning. We instead shape model perception through native attention, an intrinsic model signal. Rather than treating attention as a post-hoc explanation, we formulate it as a reward-anchored, token-aligned routing signal that encourages attention patterns supporting visually grounded reasoning. To this end, we propose NAS-PO, a Native Attention-Strategy Policy Optimization framework. NAS-PO analyzes native attention with a visual mass policy that quantifies visual allocation and a conditional visual policy that captures spatial routing over visual tokens, making the optimization well-posed. Based on this, Native Attention-Strategy Advantage Shaping (NAS-AS) draws on the visual-mass policy to redistribute signed policy-gradient credit for visually engaged rollouts. The Adaptive Attention-Coupled (AAC) loss then aligns the visual-mass policy on all response tokens and the conditional visual policy on visually active ones, consolidating the visual-routing patterns that drive successful trajectories. Experiments demonstrate that NAS-PO substantially surpasses leading RL-tuned VLMs across 7 out-of-domain reasoning and perception benchmarks, with its effectiveness consistently validated across 8B and 32B. Visual-Routing Sensitivity (VRS) analysis further indicates that the optimized attention prioritizes visually load-bearing regions.

Cross-Dataset Generalization of Glaucoma Detection CNNs: Effects of Operating Threshold, Multi-Source Training, and Color Normalization

Deep learning models for glaucoma detection are usually tested on images from the same dataset used for training. That says little about how they will work at a new hospital or with a new camera. We fine-tuned five ImageNet-pretrained CNNs (ResNet50, DenseNet121, MobileNetV2, EfficientNet-B0, InceptionV3) with identical settings on one of three public fundus datasets (ACRIMA, ORIGA, RIM-ONE DL). Each was tested on a held-out part of its own dataset and on the other two datasets in full. We used three random seeds and report mean and SD over seeds. Mean in-domain AUC was 0.913 and cross-source AUC was 0.702, a drop of 0.211 (SD 0.030; 95% CI 0.135 to 0.286). After correction for multiple comparisons we found no evidence that any architecture did better, though three seeds give low power. At thresholds set on the source validation data, specificity fell from 0.870 to 0.506 and sensitivity from 0.831 to 0.726. Resetting the threshold on 10% of the target images raised specificity to 0.670 but lowered sensitivity to 0.609, and balanced accuracy rose only from 0.616 to 0.639. Training on two sources together did not help (held-out AUC 0.695 versus 0.702). LAB reference normalization (cross-source AUC 0.755) and strong color augmentation (0.754) gave similar gains, cutting the drop to about 0.16. Ben Graham normalization (0.733) and CLAHE (0.712) helped less, and gray-world (0.696) did not help. The two best methods raised specificity but lowered sensitivity. On G1020, used only for testing, AUC stayed near 0.5 in every condition, and networks trained within G1020 were also close to chance. Strong in-domain results do not show that a glaucoma model will work on new data. Normalization and color augmentation reduce the drop but do not remove it. Studies should report external validation and check the operating threshold, not only AUC.

Synthetic Realities: Evaluating Human Ability to Distinguish AIGenerated Videos from Real Footage

Recent advances in generative AI have enabled multiple pathways for high-fidelity video synthesis: text-to-video, image-to-video animation, and video outpainting. Empirical side-by-side evaluations of how untrained viewers perceive and distinguish these outputs from real footage remain scarce. In this study, we systematically compare human detection accuracy across these three AI generation techniques within three thematic contexts: historical footage, film and media content, and natural environments. We constructed a balanced stimulus set comprising equal amounts of real and AI-generated videos (18 each). The AI clips were evenly distributed across the three generation methods using Google's Veo 3, Lightricks LTX Video, and Wan VACE. All videos were produced and standardized within the ComfyUI framework to ensure consistent quality and duration. 87 participants judged every clip in a binary forced-choice task ("real" vs. "AI-generated"). Participants correctly identified videos 60% of the time on average. Image-to-video clips were recognized most accurately (79%), followed by real footage (64%), outpainting (49%), and text-to-video (43%). Accuracy also varied by theme: film and historical scenes yielded higher detection rates than environmental clips, which were frequently mistaken for AI. Logistic regression confirmed significant effects of both technique and theme as well as their interaction (p < 0.001), indicating that detection success depends jointly on how the content was generated and what it depicts. Findings reveal a consistent bias toward assuming synthetic origins and highlight that perceptual realism in AI video is shaped more by context than by model type, underscoring the importance of media-literacy approaches and context-aware evaluation tools for navigating increasingly synthetic visual media.

SurgStream: A Workflow-Anchored Proactive Streaming Framework and Benchmark for Real-Time Surgical Video Understanding

Surgical video understanding is essential for intraoperative assistance such as workflow monitoring, instrument preparation and procedural documentation. Delivering such assistance in real time requires a model to jointly perceive the ongoing scene, remember past surgical events, and respond before clinically relevant moments pass. Existing approaches provide limited support for jointly retaining procedural evidence and deciding when to respond in streaming surgical video. In this work, we propose SurgStream, a workflow-anchored proactive streaming framework for real-time surgical video understanding. We first introduce Workflow-Anchored Memory (WAM) to record structured instrument-action-target observations and fold them into phase-level summaries, providing compact and queryable surgical memory. Deadline-aware Transition Learning (DTL) is then devised to supervise response states within clinical windows, enabling timely responses before procedural deadlines. We further introduce SurgStream-Bench, comprising 11.7K streaming interactions across various tasks covering real-time perception, backward memory queries, forward proactive QA, proactive alerts and caption memory. Experiments on SurgStream-Bench show gains over state-of-the-art methods, especially in memory and forward proactive QA. The source code will be available.

Multimodal BVP and Respiration for Automated Pain Recognition with Hankel-FCN

Automated pain recognition is a fundamental challenge for the advancement of clinical care and empathetic human-computer interaction, due to the subjective and dynamic nature of pain. In this study, we propose a deep learning framework for objective pain assessment based on multimodal physiological signals from the AI4Pain dataset. The approach focuses on Blood Volume Pulse (BVP) and Respiration (RESP), capturing complementary aspects of autonomic responses to nociceptive stimulation. To explicitly model their temporal dynamics, the signals are transformed into structured trajectory representations using Hankel matrix embeddings, enabling the learning of dynamical patterns beyond conventional time-series representations. These embeddings are processed by modality-specific Fully Convolutional Networks, and their outputs are integrated through a late fusion strategy for three-class classification (No Pain, Low Pain, High Pain). Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method achieves an accuracy of 67.09% and an F1-score of 66.41%, providing competitive performance compared to existing approaches, while relying on no handcrafted feature engineering. Multimodal fusion significantly outperformed either modality alone, and the Hankel embedding showed a trend towards further improvement. Further validation is required to establish generalisability to broader populations and clinical settings.

MUPA${}^2$S: Multimodal Unified Perception with Asymmetric Attention for Stress Assessment

Stress is a leading contributor to long-term physical and mental health decline. The automatic assessment of stress is a promising approach for continuous monitoring and mitigating human biases. Feature-level fusion does not exceed the video-only baseline, while unified input-level integration provides a parameter-efficient mechanism for jointly processing the two modalities.

Knowledge-Guided Floodwater Level Reasoning for Cloud-Edge Collaborative Monitoring

Frequent urban floodwater poses a serious threat to the safety and efficiency of intelligent transportation systems. Existing vision-based methods for floodwater level detection face two main limitations. First, the lack of explicit reasoning leads to poor detection performance and little interpretability. Second, these methods struggle to generalize to the complex and varied real-world road scenes seen from different viewpoints. To address this, we reformulate the task as an explicit visual reasoning problem and propose HydroReasoner, a floodwater level reasoning framework designed for cloud-edge collaboration. HydroReasoner builds on Qwen2.5-VL and is trained with Group Relative Policy Optimization. To address the scarcity of reasoning process annotations, we build a floodwater domain knowledge graph HydroKG and design a novel KG path reward. Through deterministic graph traversal over HydroKG, this reward supervises the model's reasoning process at a fine granularity, which improves both reasoning and generalization. For edge deployment, we apply 4-bit quantization to the model, cutting GPU memory usage by 57% and inference latency by 43% while out-of-domain accuracy drops by only 0.2 percentage points. We also propose a confidence-based cloud-edge routing mechanism, which improves system safety and eases the computational load on the cloud. In addition, we build HydroLevel, a floodwater benchmark dataset of 3,000 images, for training and evaluating the model. Experimental results show that HydroReasoner achieves detection accuracy 4.0 and 13.6 percentage points higher than conventional vision baselines in the in-domain and out-of-domain evaluations respectively, with performance dropping by only 4.4 percentage points out of domain.

Object-Only Is Not Context-Free: A Counterfactual Diagnostic for Context Removal in Object Retrieval

Object-centric retrieval pipelines often mask the background before pooling backbone features, aiming for a descriptor that reflects the object rather than its scene. Whether this truly yields an object-only descriptor, however, is rarely tested. We introduce the Keyhole Aperture Probe (KAP), a counterfactual diagnostic that repurposes the ground-truth masks of existing panoptic segmentation datasets (COCO, ADE20K) for retrieval: it holds the pooled object region fixed and varies only the pixels outside it, summarizing a backbone's context dependence in a single number. We find that the pooled object region has already integrated its surround. Replacing the context, with a neutral fill or even a different real scene, changes the descriptor, and when retrieval fails under a blank fill, the descriptor matches plain-background images rather than its own object. Object-only is not context-free. Across ten modern backbones, object-only descriptors retain only about a quarter to two-thirds of their full-image accuracy, and the effect is not unique to Transformer self-attention: a convolutional backbone shows it too. The dependence is also reducible: a lightweight adapter that encourages the descriptor to stay stable as context is removed lowers it and improves full-image instance retrieval on INSTRE, transferring to ILIAS with accuracy improved or preserved. A matched contrastive-fine-tuning control does not reproduce those gains. We release KAP as a simple, backbone-agnostic evaluation protocol to measure and reduce this dependence before deployment.

Event-Based Optical Flow Constraint via Time-Sliced Plane Fitting

Event-based optical flow estimation using spatiotemporal planar approximation inherently assumes constant velocity and tends to average out temporal velocity variations. In this paper, we propose a flow estimation method that extends this planar approximation along the temporal axis. The resulting event-based optical flow constraint allows existing variational optical flow formulations to model temporal motion variation. Specifically, we partition the space-time volume into sub-intervals and apply a two-step planar approximation to track object edges consistently. Moreover, a spatial compensation mechanism and a temporal regularization term are incorporated to compensate for edge displacements and temporally stabilize the flow estimation, respectively. The experimental results show that modeling temporal motion variation improves flow estimation accuracy over conventional methods.

Answering Without Arriving: An Evidence Audit for Embodied Question Answering

Embodied question answering asks an agent to navigate, observe, and then answer questions about what it saw, yet answer accuracy alone cannot tell whether any of this happened. A plausible answer can come from the question, the room label, or the answer choices, and a correct answer does not show that the supporting observation was ever made. We present a multi-room benchmark whose 200 test episodes and 800 questions are stratified by route extent, with an evaluation ladder that holds the questions fixed while varying only the evidence supplied to the answerer, from text-only probes through closed-loop agents to replay of the reference trajectory. Questions are generated from symbolic records of rooms, visible objects, and frames, so every answer can be traced to the observations that support it. A closed-loop vision-language agent reaches no target in 200 episodes yet answers 45.5% of questions, within 0.1 points of the same model given no images; replaying the reference trajectory raises the same model to 57.4%, so the evidence exists along the route but is not gathered. A family-level audit then shows that access to evidence is not the same as use of it. Four of eight families are largely answerable from text, one cites frames that cannot establish its own answer, and the oracle conditions reach 59.5% on Rooms-visited by counting the supplied images. Evidence acquisition, answer coverage, and shortcut sensitivity must therefore be established before an accuracy gain can be credited to memory.

SurgQ-VLM: Question-Conditioned Dual-Stream Compression and Answer-Type-Aware Reinforcement for Surgical Video Question Answering

Reasoning about surgical video -- which phase is underway, which instruments act, and when events occur -- underpins operative documentation, workflow analysis, and skill assessment. Yet surgical visual question answering is still largely built on single frames and closed labels that cannot express a duration, an ordering, or a count, and the video models that lift the frame limit typically compress every clip the same way regardless of the question, discarding the temporal evidence the answer needs. Worse, the answers are structurally heterogeneous, spanning five incompatible structures -- binary, classification, multi-label, counting, and sentence-temporal -- that no single training signal scores uniformly well. We propose SurgQ-VLM, a two-stage model for surgical video question answering.
