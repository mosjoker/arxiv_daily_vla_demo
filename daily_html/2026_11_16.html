Aerial Person Detection under Edge Constraints: Held-Out Evaluation and Edge-Ground Verification

A search drone sees a missing person as a few pixels, carries a small computer, and shares a narrow radio link. We study person detection under those limits; the primary evaluations were declared before they ran, and later analyses are marked exploratory. In an exploratory comparison, holding out recording blocks inferred from thermal frame identifiers cut recall at a fixed false-alarm target from 32.7% to 12.3%. With nothing tuned on test blocks, a 46,000-parameter detector gated by a contrast cue finds 10.4% of people against 5.7% for the same network applied everywhere. A standard detector, YOLO11n, trained under the same folds finds 54.5% at a similar false-alarm rate for about eleven times the arithmetic, and on our 4K field footage at 117 m has a walking person among its top 16 detections in up to 92% of frames against 74% for our board. Run alone, YOLO11n's thresholds set at one location overshot their false-alarm target 1.7 to 3.4 times at new locations, and its best configuration at altitude cost 4 to 24 standard passes per frame. Re-scoring its 16 best crops (154 KB per frame) with an 842,000-parameter ground model, thresholds frozen on a validation location, raised recall from 44.9% to 53.7% against the same 16 crops with YOLO's own scores; in a post hoc paired analysis over 15 test locations the gain was +8.8 points [+2.4, +12.5]. Pooled false detections fell from 0.86 to 0.64 per frame, but that paired change was not distinguishable from zero. Coverage of a still person in earth tones was very low for both detectors in the clips we flew.

Sequential Semantic-Non-Semantic Watermarking: Robustness Against UnMarker Attacks

Advancements in artificial intelligence (AI) have accelerated digital content generation and intensified concerns around fraud, misuse, counterfeiting, and content provenance. Defensive image watermarking provides a practical mechanism for identifying and tracing digital content, but recent watermark-removal attacks challenge its robustness. This study investigates Sequential Semantic Non-Semantic Watermarking (SSNSW), which combines HiDDeN and StegaStamp as complementary non-semantic and semantic watermarking schemes. Using 50 images from MS-COCO and CelebA-HQ, both embedding orders are evaluated, followed by UnMarker's non-semantic and semantic attacks applied individually and sequentially. Results show that both watermarks remain highly decodable after dual embedding, with only limited embedding-order effects. Under individual attacks, one watermark generally remains strongly decodable: StegaStamp under the non-semantic attack and HiDDeN under the semantic attack. Sequential attacks are therefore required to substantially reduce both watermark signals, while attack order has a limited and configuration-dependent effect. The semantic attack also shows a broader effect across the two watermark types than the non-semantic attack. LPIPS remains relatively low in several settings, although qualitative inspection reveals noticeable distortions, particularly in selected facial images, indicating that LPIPS should not be used alone to assess visual quality. Overall, the findings show that combining complementary watermark types can increase robustness against single-stage removal attacks and motivate broader multi-layer watermarking, stronger perceptual evaluation, and further study of sequential watermark-removal strategies.

Trading Parameters for Accuracy in MambaVision for Chest Radiograph Classification

MambaVision, a hybrid state-space and attention backbone, places most of its parameters in its widest, fourth stage. We quantified what that stage contributes to multi-label chest radiograph classification. The fourth stage was replaced with lighter single-scan state-space blocks adapted from Spatial-Mamba, with multi-scale dilated depthwise convolutions applied to the scan output; the first three stages were retained. Tiny, Small, and Base models were evaluated across nine dataset-scale combinations drawn from two source cohorts: the National Institutes of Health ChestX-ray14 archive (full ChestMNIST and a high-resolution subset) and Indiana University Open-i. At the Tiny scale, replacement reduced parameter count by 35.7% and increased graphics processing unit throughput by 19.3%; the savings came from the stage's dense projections, not from removing quadratic attention. Without distillation, the mean area under the receiver operating characteristic curve on ChestMNIST was 0.004 lower than the baseline (95% bootstrap confidence interval, 0.002 to 0.006), and 0.003 to 0.006 lower across all nine combinations. Knowledge distillation from an unreplaced teacher narrowed the gap to the undistilled baseline to 0.001-0.002 and met the equivalence criterion on ChestMNIST, but distilling the baseline with the same recipe improved it by 0.001-0.003, so a gap of 0.002-0.005 persisted under symmetric training. At the Base scale, the unreplaced Small model was both smaller and more accurate. Fourth-stage replacement is a useful trade-off only for Tiny and Small models. These measurements let developers of chest radiograph screening tools weigh model size and speed against a quantified loss of diagnostic discrimination.

Geo2Gcode: From 3D Geometry to CNC Programs through Structured Machining Primitives

Turning a 3D shape into a Computer Numerical Control (CNC) program is still largely manual. For every part, a machinist chooses setups, tools, step-downs, and toolpath patterns. We present Geo2Gcode, which converts a target mesh directly into standard G-code through structured machining primitives. Each primitive couples an indexed machining direction, a closed per-view reconstruction of the target, and explicit tool-radius, layer-height, and clearing-pattern parameters, and compiles deterministically into layerwise toolpaths via signed-distance queries. Most of the fidelity comes from this compiler. A lightweight selector trained with a voxel stock simulator fills in primitive parameters without per-part manual settings, approaching the finest fixed setting at about a quarter of its toolpath length. In simulation on ABC parts, Geo2Gcode reaches roughing fidelity comparable to an expert-tuned commercial CAM setup with a moderately longer toolpath, and clearly improves on a default CAM configuration. Generated programs run in a commercial G-code simulator, and one part is machined on a physical CNC mill. These results demonstrate that roughing programs can be generated directly from geometry without per-part CAM tuning.

Authors: Sangmin Hong, Dohee Cho, Mohsen Yavartanoo, Kyoung Mu Lee

Latent World Modeling for Point-Cloud Video Pretraining

Masked modeling is the dominant self-supervised paradigm for point-cloud videos. However, it leaks the information it is meant to predict, whether it reconstructs masked points as in MAE-style methods or predicts their latent features. Because point-cloud tokens are anchored at points sampled from each frame, querying a target at its own position reveals where its geometry has moved, in reconstruction and latent prediction alike. For latent predictors, random spatio-temporal masks introduce a further shortcut, as visible frames on both sides of each target allow interpolation rather than forecasting. The pretext task thus reduces to completion. Once the target positions are withheld, a masked latent predictor performs only slightly better than copying the last frame. To remove this leakage, we propose PointWM, a latent world model that turns completion into forecasting by predicting the features and geometry of future frames from strictly earlier context. Each target is queried only with its last-seen position and timestamp, while a latent action learned without annotations is meant to capture the part of the future that the past leaves undetermined. On HOI4D action segmentation with a P4Transformer backbone, PointWM improves F1@50 over training from scratch by 24.9 offline and 29.1 under strictly causal inference. These results show the promise of latent world models as a leakage-free pretraining paradigm for point-cloud videos.Our code is publicly available at https://github.com/InitalZ/PointWM.

See the Lives of Stars in a Handful of Light

Galaxies evolve over hundreds of millions of years, and no telescope has ever watched one move. Cosmological simulations make this evolution visible as a sequence of multi-view images, inviting the question of whether a galaxy can be recovered in four dimensions from its images. This setting defeats existing dynamic scene methods, since millions of stars span only a few pixels each, faint populations blend into diffuse light, and free deformations reproduce the images without obeying gravity. We present Cosmos4D, which rests on two components. Gravitational 4D Gaussian Splatting represents each star as a self-luminous Gaussian whose motion is integrated through a gravitational potential and a low-dimensional force field shared by all stars. Luminosity-stratified dynamics give bright stars individual orbits supervised across image scales and carry faint populations with a velocity field estimated from optical flow. We further build a 4D dataset of eight galaxies from four cosmological simulations, on which Cosmos4D outperforms dynamic Gaussian baselines in both photometric quality and physical accuracy. With these advancements, we glimpse eight hundred million years of a galaxy's life in ninety-six frames.

SGIP: Learning Scene-Grounded Interaction Priors for Scene-Aware Human Motion Prediction

Human motion prediction (HMP) aims to forecast future movement from an observed motion sequence, and recent methods have incorporated 3D scene geometry and object semantics to improve long-term plausibility. However, the destination of future motion remains ambiguous in cluttered scenes: the same observed motion can lead to different interaction targets or navigation modes. Existing methods either handle this ambiguity implicitly or rely on auxiliary signals like gaze that are hard to obtain in practice. To address this, we propose SGIP, which explicitly predicts where in the scene future motion will be grounded across both objects and open navigation regions, and uses it to condition future trajectory and full-body pose prediction. This scene-grounded interaction prior is learned via intent distillation: at training time only, a teacher model scores candidate scene regions by combining an LLM prompted with a textual description of the person's intent with geometric motion-scene features. A student model learns to reproduce these scores from only the observed motion and 3D scene, making intent and LLM unnecessary at test time. Across multiple benchmark datasets, SGIP outperforms prior scene-aware baselines on both trajectory and pose accuracy in a realistic deployment setting where neither gaze nor intent is available at inference, demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed framework. The project page is available at https://sgip2026.github.io.

Prompt Re-Specification and Target-Mask Availability in Mask-Conditioned Visual Reinforcement Learning for Moving-Platform Landing

Visual reinforcement learning (RL) policies that land a drone on a moving platform can fail when the landing pad looks different from the one used in training. Using binary target masks as the visual input removes most pixel-level appearance information while preserving the spatial representation of the target, and lets a text prompt specify the target. In simulation, we test whether a frozen (fixed-weight) mask-based DrQ-v2 policy can handle a new pad when only the prompt of its fine-tuned SAM 3 detector changes. A banana-themed pad lowered landing success from 75.6% to 12.0%, and SAM 3 rarely detected it under the original prompt. A revised prompt, chosen after testing several candidates, restored success to 68.4% with no weight updates. In a separate experiment with a policy trained on ground-truth masks, filling in masks that were missing while the pad was visible helped landing far more than the tested mask-centroid and area corrections. Tracking the pad over time supplied masks more often and also kept producing them after the pad was confirmed absent from the rendered image, but it did not establish a reliable improvement in landing success. Whether a usable mask reaches the policy at all thus appears to be an important limiting factor for this approach.

MARLIN: Multiscale And Directional Sparse Representation Learning for Imbalanced SAR Ship Classification

Despite recent progress in SAR maritime monitoring, ship classification remains challenging due to limited labeled data, class imbalance, large intra-class variability, fine-grained inter-class similarity, and variations in target scale, orientation, and backscattering structure. To address these challenges, this paper proposes MARLIN, a class-frequency-aware framework with directional sparse and hierarchical multi-scale feature extraction for ship type classification in SAR images. The proposed architecture first constructs two complementary representations of each input image, where each views are processed by parallel encoder branches. Each branch begins with a directional sparse convolutional stem designed to capture elongated and orientation-dependent ship scattering patterns, followed by customized hierarchical multiscale feature extraction blocks. To improve learning under imbalanced class distributions, we introduce a class-frequency aware objective. The proposed objective combines margin-based classification with class-frequency-weighted prototype compactness and class-pair frequency weighted margin separation. This formulation shapes the embedding space by encouraging samples from the same class to form compact clusters while enforcing stronger separation for class pairs involving underrepresented ship categories. Extensive experiments on three complementary datasets demonstrate that in addition to being lightweight and parameter efficient, MARLIN improves both overall accuracy and class-balanced metrics, confirming its effectiveness for robust SAR ship-type classification under limited and imbalanced training conditions. The codes are available at github.com/benyaminhosseiny/marlin.

acuSimNet: Multi-View Self-Occlusion-Awared Visibility Learning for Cranio-Cervical Acupuncture Points

The localization of acupuncture points (acupoints) in Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) presents unique challenges since they are defined by abstract principles rather than distinct anatomical landmarks. Existing approaches are typically constrained to single view or rely on indirect calculations of relative positions with respect to other landmarks, thereby overlooking human anatomical variations. Furthermore, acupoint visibility assessment, which determins whether points are occluded by human itself, hair, clothing, or other objects, has received limited attention in practical applications. Acupoint localization itself does not require 3D reconstruction, but inferring their occlusion relationships with anatomical surfaces does, which adds computational cost and limits real-time inference. In this work, we introduce acuSimNet, an efficient hierarchical multi-task learning architecture for multi-view, self-occlusion-aware visibility prediction of acupoints, achieving 99.97% accuracy on the validation set. Our approach also addresses the challenges of high-dimensional classification (174 acupoints in cervicocranial region), negative convergence issues for visible acupoints, and inter-task scheduling optimization, resulting in substantially accelerated convergence. We improved the training efficiency from the exisiting methods of 3000 epochs to achieve 99% validation accuracy, to our optimized framework achieving 90% accuracy in 39 epochs and 99% accuracy in 86 epochs. This architecture overcomes the limitations of existing methods, could enable practical applications in acupoints detection and visualization, advancing the automation of TCM.

FastGRN: Few-Step Generative Refinement Networks

Generative Refinement Networks (GRN) generate an image by repeatedly re-predicting and refining every token of a discrete latent. They reach high fidelity, but need dozens of Markov steps. We therefore train few-step GRNs on the distribution of their own samples. We use its own uniform-state corruption to perform distribution matching on discrete tokens, which lets a discrete generator learn by backpropagation, and we pair noise with samples in the manner of rectified flow. We find that the steps of a few-step GRN do not carry the same amount of information or the same repair load. We therefore exploit its discrete refinement during training and build few-step GRNs in three parts. Oversampling samples the student with more steps than it was trained for and refines its judge on these samples. Non-uniform sampling trains only the last step and hands it most of the information at sampling time. Refinement confidence keeping holds on to the tokens the student is already sure of. We train a fast class-conditional generator on ImageNet 256 and, with the same method, a 2B text-to-image model. On ImageNet 256, the 3-step FastGRN beats the FID of GRN. On a 2B binary-token text-to-image GRN, a 4-step student also nearly matches the GenEval of GRN-T2I 2B.

ReactBench-AD: Do Driving VLAs and World-Action Models React to Imminent Hazards in Their Path?

Vision-Language-Action models (VLAs) and World-Action Models (WAMs) have emerged in autonomous driving with the promise of strong generalization to long-tail scenarios. Although these models achieve state-of-the-art results on autonomous driving benchmarks, most existing benchmarks are dominated by nominal scenarios and therefore cannot test this promise. To test one such long-tail capability that is critical for safety, we introduce ReactBench-AD, a benchmark that measures how driving models react to objects that appear in the ego vehicle's path. Built on real NAVSIM and PhysicalAI-Autonomous-Vehicles scenes, ReactBench-AD composites a crossing pedestrian, a standing pedestrian, or a stationary car consistently across all camera views and history frames. Each edited scene thus forms an exact, pixel-level counterfactual pair with its original, which allows us to isolate the causal effect of the inserted object on the predicted trajectory. Evaluating four VLAs and two WAMs, we find that they react weakly, often barely at all. Finally, we test how easily one of the evaluated VLAs, ReCogDrive, can be taught to brake hard for imminent hazards: a short fine-tuning stage on edited scenes substantially improves its reactions, with the learned behavior generalizing to unseen objects and fully real scenes, at minimal cost to nominal driving. These results call into question the out-of-the-box long-tail capabilities of these models, but suggest that they can be a strong foundation for acquiring them with little additional training, highlighting the value of real or synthetic data that demonstrates the long-tail behaviors nominal datasets rarely contain. Code and data will be made publicly available.

PlanDiv: Test-Time Scaling with Latent Plans for Vision-Language-Action Models

Test-time scaling improves vision-language-action (VLA) policies by drawing several action candidates and executing the best one. We find that its returns diminish quickly when the candidates are resampled from one decoder: they differ by noise rather than by plan, and for each of three resampling sources, going from four to eight candidates adds only 0.06-0.07 to the average number of completed tasks on CALVIN. We introduce PlanDiv, an action head that makes the candidates differ by plan: a lightweight planner proposes K latent plan hypotheses, a shared masked-diffusion executor decodes one action chunk per plan from a single backbone pass, and the policy executes the chunk that its own likelihood ranks highest. The plans are learned from demonstrations alone, without plan labels or rewards. With the backbone, data and training recipe held fixed, eight plan-conditioned candidates raise five-task chain success on CALVIN ABC$\rightarrow$D from 56.9% to 69.9%, while eight candidates from autoregressive, plan-free or same-plan resampling reach at most 64.9%. The same-plan baseline is PlanDiv's own checkpoint with the plan held fixed, so this gap comes from the plans, not from drawing more samples. On LIBERO-Long, four plans remove 40% of single-candidate failures, against at most 21% for matched resampling. On a real Franka arm the gain appears where a task admits more than one solution: PlanDiv is on par with a single-output OpenVLA-OFT+ baseline on a one-solution task and succeeds in 51 of 54 trials against 40 of 54 on two tasks with two solutions.

Glance, then Select: Evidence-Tempered Visual Token Selection for Extremely Compressed Multimodal LLMs

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) encode a high-resolution image into thousands of visual tokens, which dominate the cost of the prefill stage. Visual token reduction removes most of these tokens, but two problems remain at extreme budgets. The vision encoder still processes every patch of the image, and the few surviving tokens often miss the small regions that hold the answer. In this paper, we propose Glance-ETD, a two-stage token reduction framework guided by a lightweight question-conditioned scorer. In the first stage, a glance encoder views the image at low resolution and re-encodes only the question-relevant regions at native resolution. In the second stage, Evidence-Tempered Diversity (ETD) selects the final tokens by combining diversity with the evidence predicted by the scorer, so that the kept tokens cover the scene and retain the answer. A single-rollout self-distillation recipe trains the MLLM with the glance encoder. With only 5% of the visual tokens, Glance-ETD retains 91.6% of the full-token performance of Qwen3.5-4B across fourteen benchmarks and outperforms the strongest prior method, LT-OPD, by 17.3 points and the same training recipe with DivPrune by 6.0. It also speeds up the time to first token on 4K images by 3.1x and generalizes to three other MLLMs and to high-resolution document benchmarks, with the largest gains on text-rich tasks.

What Matters for Cross-Modal Alignment

The Platonic Representation Hypothesis posits that models trained on different modalities converge toward a shared representation. Related work aligns frozen image and text encoders on natural images without paired data. We test zero-pair alignment on five datasets across four modalities: natural images, environmental audio, chest radiographs, and 3D computed tomography (CT). It succeeds only on COCO and fails on the other four. We analyze the failures and find that alignment must first locate the subspace where the modalities co-vary and then orient the mapping within it. Consistent with previous studies, medical imaging is the hardest case: its shared subspace carries the smallest fraction of the variance. When this fraction is small, alignment needs image-text pairs, and random-matrix theory predicts how many pairs are needed. These findings motivate LightAlign: a few pairs let a ridge regression between two frozen encoders locate and orient the mapping simultaneously, and a classifier fitted only on report text then predicts findings from the mapped images. On 3D CT, LightAlign surpasses models trained end to end on image-report pairs without finding-level labels: with only 200 pairs, LightAlign outperforms CT-CLIP on CT-RATE (0.754 vs. 0.733 AUROC), and with only 100 CT-RATE pairs, it does so on RAD-ChestCT, an out-of-distribution dataset from a different hospital. On CheXpert, it reaches 0.862 mean AUROC with only about 5% as many pairs as models trained on MIMIC-CXR.

These findings demonstrate accurate, efficient KL grading and potential adaptation to new populations with limited annotation.

Morphology-Decoupled Segmentation for Multiple Tunnel Defects Under Complex Environments

Automated tunnel-lining inspection must segment different defect geometries: cracks are thin, elongated, and connectivity-sensitive, whereas leakage, lining falling off, and segment damage are spatially extensive and context-dependent. Existing approaches generally apply a common decoder across defect classes or introduce boundary, topology, and directional mechanisms as isolated enhancements. This paper introduces Morphology-Decoupled Segmentation (MDS), in which defect morphology becomes the organising principle for feature routing, decoding, and interaction. A supervised routing module partitions multiscale encoder features into complementary linear and areal streams. The linear stream employs Deformable Strip Attention with image-conditioned direction hypotheses to preserve crack continuity without assuming fixed horizontal or vertical orientations, while the areal stream combines Pyramid Pooling with feature-pyramid fusion to capture diffuse boundaries and regional context. The representations are coupled through gated cross-morphology attention, allowing complementary information exchange while retaining morphology-specific features. A resolution-adaptive tiling and probability-blending pipeline preserves fine defects across variable-resolution imagery. MDS achieves 81.17% mIoU on the internal validation set and improves foreground image-level recognition over a backbone-matched SegDINO model, primarily for areal defects. Controlled ablations demonstrate the complementary contributions of routing supervision and cross-stream interaction while revealing a trade-off between areal-class performance and crack specialisation. The submitted model achieved 67.55% mIoU on an organiser-controlled hidden test, and the team placed second. Selective dynamic INT8 quantisation reduced model storage by 2.83 times with negligible accuracy loss.

CreaTrace: Video Creative Plagiarism Detection and Evidence Tracing

Video creative plagiarism refers to the unauthorized reproduction of creative ideas across videos despite substantial differences in visual appearance, posing a new challenge to video copyright protection. However, existing video copy detection methods primarily focus on visual content similarity, struggling to capture abstract creative that are entangled with specific visual realizations. To address this issue, we introduce a novel task, named Video Creative Plagiarism Detection (VCPD), and propose an iterative creative evidence tracing framework, termed CreaTrace, to verify creative similarity and accurately localize the corresponding evidence segments. Specifically, we design a Multimodal Semantic Event Segmentation strategy to divide similar long videos into coherent narrative events, facilitating event-level dataset construction and retrieving similar segments. Based on these events, CreaTrace employs Coarse Creative Matching to identify candidate event pairs through narrative semantics and camera language. Then CreaTrace presents Creative Decoupling to disentangle abstract creative patterns from specific visual realizations. Meanwhile, Creative Swap Contrastive Learning is devised to enhance creative consistency through cross-realization feature swapping. Benefiting from the above designs, our CreaTrace enables accurate creative similarity detection and temporal localization, while providing interpretable evidence report. Extensive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of CreaTrace across diverse creative plagiarism scenarios.

FinVision: Multimodal AI for Visualizing Financial Narratives

Financial narratives contain rich information about firms' performance and business conditions. Overall, our findings show how domain-adapted multimodal generative AI can create a new visual representation of financial text and provide an additional modality for studying business communication.

Two-Stage Prompt Learning: A Unified Framework for Person ReID without Clothing Hypothesis

Person re-identification (ReID) in real-world scenarios involves three clothes conditions: the same clothes (SC), changed clothes (CC), and the unknown (GEN) scenario where clothes changes are not specified. Existing methods typically train separate SC- or CC-specific models, which limits generalization across all conditions. Meanwhile, pre-trained visual-language models (VLMs) such as CLIP have shown strong generalization ability in various vision tasks, motivating recent ReID methods to incorporate textual prompts. However, to distinguish SC and CC, prior approaches rely on fixed predefined instructions, which lack learnable context embeddings in prompts and cannot effectively handle the GEN scenario. To overcome these limitations, we propose Two-Stage Prompt Learning (TSPL), a unified framework that integrates clothes-identity decoupling and scenario-aware task prompts within VLMs. In Stage 1, appearance-decomposed prompt learning disentangles clothes and identity information under frozen encoders, producing stable embeddings where clothes cues can be selectively preserved or suppressed. In Stage 2, task-aware prompt learning trains the image encoder with learnable context embeddings guided by Stage-1 representations, enabling robust adaptation across SC, CC, and GEN conditions. Extensive experiments on five benchmarks show that TSPL achieves state-of-the-art performance under all scenarios, improving mAP by up to 9.2% on LTCC (SC) and by 6.4% on VC-Clothes (GEN), respectively. The code will be released at https://github.com/YuSuen/TSPL.
