BAL: Bidirectional Autoregression in Latent Space for Learning Human Movement Representations

Learning semantically meaningful human movement representations from 3D pose sequences is essential for human behavior modeling. Existing self-supervised methods derive supervision from artificial assumptions, e.g., invariance to augmentation within a certain range or recoverability of masked regions at a certain temporal scale, introducing heuristic hyperparameters that make the learned semantics sensitive to their settings. We propose BAL, a self-supervised framework that instead exploits supervision intrinsic to human movement: the natural ordering structure of everyday behavior. BAL autoregressively predicts in a semantically abstract latent space, analogous to how humans anticipate others' actions rather than their precise joint configurations. Bidirectional autoregressive prediction, both forward and backward in time, fully leverages these ordering constraints while enabling representation extraction using both past and future context at inference. To suppress representation collapse, BAL decomposes each prediction target into a global component capturing sequence-level semantics and a local component capturing context-independent movement. We prove that the local targets retain nontrivial angular diversity unless the representations are completely collapsed, preserving a meaningful training signal. Experiments on large-scale datasets demonstrate that BAL's representations are highly effective for motion captioning, forecasting, and interpolation, outperforming baselines on most evaluation metrics.

Restoring Diversity in Distilled Image and Video Generators by Probing Activations

Distilling diffusion models into few-step student models substantially reduces sampling latency, but induces severe diversity collapse: samples generated from different random seeds show minimal variation. In this work, we investigate the root cause of this failure by probing the internal representations of student models relative to their teachers. We find that distilled students maintain total activation variance comparable to their teachers, but concentrate it into substantially fewer directions. This directional collapse is localized to an identifiable intermediate block at the initial denoising step. Consequently, diversity collapse stems from a misallocation of activation variance rather than an inherent loss of generative capacity. Guided by this finding, we propose ReDiverse to reinstate the missing variance directions. At training time, ReDiverse-FT fine-tunes a lightweight adapter that regresses student activations onto the teacher's, reviving the teacher's feature variance characteristic. At inference time, ReDiverse-TF introduces a training-free mechanism that steers activations along precomputed teacher principal directions. Across image and video distillation benchmarks, ReDiverse consistently restores sample diversity while preserving generation quality, further supported by the user study.

JVT: Just Video Transformer makes pixel-space video diffusion simple

Direct diffusion modeling of high-dimensional raw video data is challenging, and existing pixel-space methods often rely on specialized architectures. We introduce JVT (Just Video Transformer), a simple large-patch DiT-style Transformer for pixel-space video diffusion, without a pretrained autoencoder, auxiliary detailers, cascaded stages, or U-Net-style skip connections. We systematically examine video patch embeddings, register tokens, and adaptive layer normalization to identify design choices that balance generation quality and computational efficiency. The resulting JVT design achieves generation quality competitive with latent diffusion baselines in both class-conditioned and unconditional settings. The pixel-space formulation of JVT also naturally supports joint generation of pixel-aligned modalities. We demonstrate this capability through camera-conditioned RGB-disparity generation, which improves camera following relative to RGB-only baselines and yields explicit scene geometry without an additional reconstruction model at inference. These results highlight the potential of simple DiT-style Transformers for pixel-space video diffusion, and we hope our findings offer useful insights and practical guidance for future research.

Authors: Jianjun Zhou, Hua Geng, Haoyu Guo

Understanding Contextual Bias in Agricultural CNNs Through Hidden-Neuron Concept Induction

Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) can classify images using background-dominated patches that contain little or none of the target object, showing that they exploit contextual information. Previous studies detected this behavior through classification accuracy but say little about what CNN hidden layers represent. We investigate whether Concept Induction can reveal interpretable visual properties associated with hidden-neuron activations on contextual image patches. Using five agricultural datasets with high, moderate, and low contextual bias, we analyze four CNN architectures at three network depths and five patch locations. Quantitative image descriptors of each patch are encoded in a knowledge graph and used as background knowledge for Concept Induction, which derives logical expressions characterizing neuron activation. These expressions are tested on held-out images using the Mann-Whitney U test and Cliff's delta. Of 48,013 tested neuron-feature pairs, 13,164 (27.4%) were validated. Validation was most frequent at intermediate depths (mean 40.2% at mid layers versus 12.0% at deep layers), and no patch location dominated across datasets. Among the tested descriptors, edge, Gabor, and Tamura texture features had the highest validation rates. Stronger contextual bias did not correspond to higher neuron-feature validation rates: moderately biased Sugarcane had the highest rate (40.5%) and highly biased Cassava the lowest (19.2%). These results suggest that behavioral context dependence and the semantic interpretability of hidden neurons capture different aspects of CNN behavior, and that Concept Induction can complement patch-based bias detection.

Authors: Abhilekha Dalal, Avishek Das, Samatha Ereshi Akkamahadevi

EventSpikeSeg: Memory-Aware Mixed-Signal Architecture for Event-Camera Spiking Segmentation

Event-camera segmentation requires persistent neural state despite sparse input events, making memory organization a central constraint on embedded implementation. We present EventSpikeSeg, a mixed-signal architecture combining resident capacitor memory with shared analog processing in GF180MCU 180-nm CMOS. Its 25-population spiking encoder-decoder has 143,824 synaptic weights but requires 30,976,000 membrane states at 440 x 640 resolution. The mapping specifies chronological 5-ms model updates, digital event/address services and 64-state capacitor banks. Characterization covers 22 module families and 74 operating points, distinguishing transistor simulations from digital-library estimates. Nominal results include 41.711 microwatts biased neuron-core power, 3.338-pJ positive terminal energy for a 1.4-microsecond cell read, and 0.531-mV selected-state drift over a 5-ms extracted-tile hold. Commit and coupling errors are reported separately. The software checkpoint records 27.976% mean intersection-over-union on a held-out six-class DSEC split; this is not hardware accuracy. Component layouts and simulation evidence quantify local implementation costs and expose storage replication and service scheduling as scaling constraints. A detailed SPICE-calibrated behavioral forward simulation of one full-resolution DSEC frame achieves 24.26% mean intersection-over-union, versus 26.22% for the matched PyTorch reference, using a compact 5-fF bank variant and external count/voltage calibration. Physical full-network operation, system energy and mismatch robustness remain unverified.

Authors: Amir Mohammad Khadem Hosseini, Sattar Mirzakuchaki

Box-conditional Hypothesis Testing for Out-of-Distribution Object Detection

Detecting out-of-distribution (OOD) objects is essential for ensuring the reliability and safety of object detectors in real-world deployment. We treat the decision on a detected box as a hypothesis test with the null hypothesis that the box covers an object of an in-distribution (ID) class. The null distribution changes with the size of the box and its contact with the image border, and the detector observes both. The test can therefore rank a box only against ID boxes of the same size and border contact, which gives a distribution-free bound on the false-alarm rate for each of them and uncovers the separation that one null distribution hides. The power of the test also rests on the layer it reads. Layers built only for the ID classes have no reason to keep what sets an unknown object apart, so the test reads the features before them. We propose \textbf{PLUM}, a training-free score that pools the last backbone stage inside the box and ranks its distance to the ID classes among ID boxes of the same size and border contact. On the standard benchmark, PLUM reaches the highest AUROC among the compared methods without external models, with 91.5 and 94.5 on PASCAL-VOC and 95.1 and 97.5 on BDD100K. Authors: Donghoon Lee, Jusung Park, Shinjin Kang

StreamEmbed: Real-Time Indexing from Pixels for Screenshot Retrieval

Screen history can serve as searchable visual memory for people and agents, but unlike an offline corpus, it must be encoded and indexed as each screenshot arrives. OCR-based indexing is fast but loses visual information; visual encoding takes over two seconds. Yet screens change incrementally: most pixels repeat within and across screenshots. This raises a question: do visual encoders need to recompute their representations from scratch for every new screenshot? We present StreamEmbed, a system for incremental visual embedding that exploits this redundancy. Guided solely by pixel differences, it combines spatial token pruning, which skips blocks that repeat a neighbor, with temporal KV reuse, which reuses cached keys and values for blocks unchanged across screenshots. A small learned adapter compensates for pruning and reuse to preserve fidelity to full-screenshot embeddings. On ScreenMemEval, our benchmark of 10,426 questions over 184 hours of screen histories from two humans and a computer-use agent, StreamEmbed sets a new accuracy-latency Pareto frontier on an M2 Pro GPU: it encodes up to 3.7x faster than full-screenshot encoding without sacrificing accuracy and improves accuracy over OCR-based indexing by up to 6.3 Recall@10 points at matched latency. A memory-efficient vector index stores a simulated year of screenshots in 0.13 GB of RAM. Together, these results make real-time indexing of screen history in its native visual form practical, laying a foundation for fully local visual assistants.
